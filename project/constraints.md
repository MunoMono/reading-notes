# Constraints for notes + AI assistance (rigour / anti-hallucination / synthesis)

These rules exist to keep the thesis on track, keep notes writeable rather than bloated, maintain parity with Zotero, and ensure that critical reading develops into defensible doctoral argument rather than descriptive summary.

## 1) Non-negotiables
- Every note must point to the **North star**:
  - **Project RQ (verbatim)**
  - **One primary theoretical-framework area**:
    - **1. Critical design historiography**
    - **2. Critical archival theory**
    - **3. Critical computational approaches**
    - **4. Feminist + situated knowledge**
  - **One literature cluster**:
    - **a) Canon + intellectual lineage**
    - **b) Operational literature**
    - **c) Contemporary bridge literature**
  - **Zotero filing path**: `Theoretical framework / [primary area] / [literature cluster]`
- Every note has **one primary theoretical-framework area** and **one Zotero literature cluster**.
- The current project/output tag is **Theoretical framework**.
- Do **not** use the Zotero placeholder folders **d) Z** or **e) ADD** in reading-note metadata.
- If a source genuinely crosses areas, choose the **single best primary filing location** for Zotero and describe the cross-over in the note rather than creating an ambiguous primary classification.
- If something does not serve the RQ/theoretical framework, write: **“OUT OF SCOPE (why)”** and stop.

## 2) Evidence rule (no hallucination)
- Do **not** state factual or interpretative claims about a source without:
  - a page reference, or
  - a clear marker: **TODO (needs page / verification)**.
- If you are unsure, write **TODO** rather than inventing.
- Do not infer an author's position from a chapter title, abstract, secondary citation, or isolated quotation where the full argument has not been checked.
- Do not make a stronger claim than the cited evidence supports.

## 3) Critical-reading claim standard
- A substantive reading note must normally contain **at least 6 critical claims**.
- A major canonical source should normally yield **6–8 critical claims**, and may exceed this where the source genuinely warrants it.
- Do **not** manufacture, split, or pad claims merely to hit a quota. If the source cannot support six genuinely distinct claims, record the supported claims and state: **“INSUFFICIENT DISTINCT EVIDENCE FOR 6 CLAIMS”**.
- Each critical claim must be written as a **short analytical paragraph**, not as a fragmentary bullet.
- Each paragraph must perform this sequence:
  - **Claim** — the proposition being advanced.
  - **Evidence** — the specific passage, example, distinction, case, or material on which the claim rests, with page number.
  - **Warrant** — why that evidence supports the claim.
  - **Boundary** — what the evidence does not establish, including relevant limits or alternative readings.
  - **Consequence** — what this changes for the thesis, theoretical framework, archive, practice, or research instrument.
- Claims should not merely restate the author. They must expose the reasoning that connects evidence to interpretation.

## 4) Voice separation: author, evidence, researcher
For every claim, keep three epistemic levels distinct:
- **Author claim** — what the source explicitly argues.
- **Evidence-supported claim** — what the cited material actually warrants.
- **Researcher inference** — what I extend, infer, or propose for the DDR thesis.

Do not collapse these into one voice. Where an inference goes beyond the source, label it clearly as **my inference**, **working proposition**, or **TODO (test against DDR evidence)**.

## 5) Practice interleave (mandatory)
- For each claim include a **Practice cross-check**:
  - Where my material supports, complicates, or resists this claim (practice note ID / archive reference / oral-history reference / computational test).
- If not available: **TODO (add practice cross-check)**.
- A literature claim must never become a claim about DDR merely because it is theoretically attractive; the archive or practice must do the evidential work.

## 6) Synthesis across the literature
- Every substantive reading note must end with a **synthesis paragraph**, not a summary list.
- The synthesis paragraph must answer:
  - What does this source change about how I understand the research problem?
  - Which part of the primary theoretical lens does it strengthen, complicate, or delimit?
  - Where does it connect to, diverge from, or leave work for another literature or theoretical area?
  - What new proposition does it make possible for the thesis, and what still has to be demonstrated empirically?
- Where no cross-source comparison is yet available, write **TODO (cross-source synthesis)** rather than inventing one.
- The aim is cumulative synthesis: each note should become reusable thesis argument, not an isolated source summary.

## 7) No antithesis / no “thesis fog”
- Do not write decorative “this is not about…” lists.
- Replace them with:
  - **Boundary**: where the claim or framework stops being useful.
  - **Risk**: what confusion, overreach, or category error could arise if it is misused.
- Boundaries must be specific to the source and argument, not generic caveats.

## 8) Definitions + benchmarks (ownership)
- If the source introduces a key term I might use, write:
  - **Term → how I will use it (my words) → page if defined**.
- Every note must include **My benchmark**:
  - 1–2 criteria for when I will treat a claim/framework as usable for my thesis.
- If I adapt an author's term, distinguish the author's definition from my thesis-specific working definition.

## 9) Style rules
- Prefer **plain language, precise sentences, and analytical paragraphs**.
- Use **first person** when it prevents ambiguity (“I use this to…”, “I infer…”, “I will test…”).
- Avoid jargon unless it is defined and necessary.
- Avoid reverential or dismissive language. Critique the argument, evidence, scope, and consequences.
- Bullets are acceptable for metadata, definitions, page capture, and checklists; **critical claims themselves should be prose paragraphs**.
- Write at least **one reusable thesis sentence** per note.

## 10) Citation discipline (Chicago NB ready)
- Capture:
  - key pages
  - one short quote (≤2 lines)
  - one accurate paraphrase
  - “first full note” + “short note form” (even if drafting uses a different rendering)
- Page numbers must attach to the proposition they support, not merely to the end of a long paragraph containing several different claims.
- If a paragraph contains multiple evidential moves from different pages, cite each move precisely.

## 11) Allowed sources in a note
You may only use:
- The source being read (with page refs), and
- North star constants (`project/north-star.yml`), and
- My own practice material (explicitly referenced),
- Otherwise: **TODO**.

Do not silently import general knowledge or another scholar's interpretation into a source note. Cross-source synthesis is allowed only when the additional source is explicitly identified and available.

## 12) Zotero parity rule
- Filing metadata in the note must map directly to the Zotero structure:
  - `Theoretical framework`
  - one of the four primary theoretical-framework areas
  - one of the three literature clusters (a/b/c)
- The note should make the intended Zotero destination explicit enough that the source can be filed without reinterpretation.
- **d) Z** and **e) ADD** are placeholders only and must never be selected by the note-taking system.

## 13) Definition of done (for a reading note)
A note is “done” when it contains:
- Thesis job filled
- Primary theoretical-framework area selected
- Literature cluster selected (a/b/c)
- Zotero filing path stated
- Project/output tag set to **Theoretical framework**
- **At least 6 substantive critical claims** completed, unless the source is explicitly marked **INSUFFICIENT DISTINCT EVIDENCE FOR 6 CLAIMS**
- Each claim written as an analytical paragraph using **Claim → Evidence → Warrant → Boundary → Consequence**
- Author claim / evidence-supported claim / researcher inference kept distinct
- Practice cross-check included for each claim (or TODO)
- At least 1 definition updated/confirmed (or “none”)
- Boundary + risk captured at source level where useful
- Final synthesis paragraph completed
- Primary RQ contribution stated explicitly
- Secondary RQ relevance stated where applicable
- Chicago payload captured
