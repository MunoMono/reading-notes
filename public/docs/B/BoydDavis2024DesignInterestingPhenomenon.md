# Thesis job

**How this source moves the primary research question forward:** Boyd Davis reconstructs a less canonical DDR lineage around George Mallen, Patrick Purcell, Gillian Patterson, Pierre Goumain and others, showing how computing, cybernetics, empirical observation, subjective data and organisational modelling became part of design research.

**How this source bears on the secondary question:** It identifies a set of DDR-period ideas with strong contemporary resonance: human–computer interaction, recursive modelling, user requirements, multidimensional analysis, social context and the difference between prescriptive and descriptive models.

**Why I’m reading this now:** It is the contemporary bridge text in this cluster and directly addresses the historiographic problem of recovering distributed DDR contributions from interviews, publications and dispersed archives.

# Position + moment

Boyd Davis asks what George Mallen brought to DDR between 1971 and 1983 and how both Mallen and the RCA changed. The chapter explicitly prioritises design, computing, cybernetics and systems thinking while reconstructing institutional relationships and intellectual contributions from oral and archival evidence. [@BoydDavis2024DesignInterestingPhenomenon, pp. 1–3]

# The author’s main move

The chapter argues that Mallen broadened DDR's computational and methodological work toward empirical modelling of real design activity, subjectivity, organisations, social context and human–computer interaction, and that recovering this contribution requires a distributed historiography rather than an Archer-only account. [@BoydDavis2024DesignInterestingPhenomenon, pp. 1–20]

# Six-claim evidence ledger

## Claim 1
- **Claim (plain):** Boyd Davis positions Mallen as extending DDR beyond a narrow design-methods programme through cybernetics, systems thinking and computing.
- **Author claim:** The chapter contrasts Archer's strong concern with design methods with Mallen's stated interest in the “broader implications” and traces Mallen's prior work in computing, Paskian cybernetics, simulation and organisational decision processes.
- **Evidence-supported claim:** Pages 1–3 document Mallen's statement about moving beyond Archer's methods focus and connect his RCA contribution to Pask's conversation theory, system dynamics and human–machine interaction.
- **Researcher inference:** DDR's computational history should be reconstructed as a network of intellectual lineages and actors rather than attributed to a single founder.
- **Evidence (quote/paraphrase + page):** Mallen recalls that Archer's “business was design methods” while he wanted to develop broader implications; Boyd Davis then traces cybernetic and simulation influences entering his RCA work. [@BoydDavis2024DesignInterestingPhenomenon, pp. 1–3]
- **Warrant (my words):** Different intellectual backgrounds create different research questions even inside one department, so institutional identity cannot be inferred from the head's published programme alone.
- **Boundary:** The account is reconstructed retrospectively and relies partly on Mallen's later testimony.
- **Consequence:** The thesis should treat DDR as an ecology of actors and intellectual imports, with attribution established trace by trace.

## Claim 2
- **Claim (plain):** The chapter makes Gillian Patterson's information work historically visible as part of DDR's research infrastructure.
- **Author claim:** Boyd Davis identifies Patterson as a central figure who progressed from secretary to Information Research Officer and Research Fellow, was described as a data analyst, and was named by Mallen alongside Purcell as one of the key figures in his RCA world.
- **Evidence-supported claim:** Page 4 gives this progression and explicitly links Patterson to information finding/handling and user specifications.
- **Researcher inference:** Formal job titles and later canonical authorship may understate research labour that was crucial to the operation of DDR projects.
- **Evidence (quote/paraphrase + page):** Boyd Davis records Patterson's role progression and Mallen's retrospective statement that Purcell and Patterson were key figures for him. [@BoydDavis2024DesignInterestingPhenomenon, p. 4]
- **Warrant (my words):** A person can exercise substantial epistemic labour through information analysis and specification even where conventional historiography privileges named principal investigators or publication authors.
- **Boundary:** Mallen's recollection does not by itself establish the full extent, authorship or influence of Patterson's work; those require primary records.
- **Consequence:** Gendered and support-role attribution should be tested through project documents, reports and acknowledgements rather than inferred from hierarchy alone.

## Claim 3
- **Claim (plain):** A key DDR shift is the explicit distinction between normative models of what designers should do and descriptive models of what they actually do.
- **Author claim:** Boyd Davis reports that the Computer Analysis project treated normative models as rationally deduced conceptual models but required descriptive models to be grounded in empirical observation.
- **Evidence-supported claim:** Page 7 states the distinction directly and interprets it as a possible marker of movement from prescriptive checklists toward engagement with live design complexity.
- **Researcher inference:** This distinction offers a precise lens for classifying DDR models without conflating prescription with observation.
- **Evidence (quote/paraphrase + page):** The report distinguishes “descriptive models” capturing actual design processes from “normative models” specifying what decisions should be made. [@BoydDavis2024DesignInterestingPhenomenon, p. 7]
- **Warrant (my words):** A model's evidential status depends on whether it claims to describe observed behaviour or prescribe preferred behaviour.
- **Boundary:** Boyd Davis cautiously says the distinction “seems possible” as evidence of a historical shift; the source does not prove a clean departmental transition.
- **Consequence:** The thesis should label DDR models by evidential function and avoid treating normative diagrams as records of actual practice.
- **Practice cross-check:** TODO (classify selected DDR diagrams/reports as descriptive, normative or mixed, with source evidence).

## Claim 4
- **Claim (plain):** Mallen's modelling programme combines computation with iterative empirical observation rather than treating requirements as fixed inputs.
- **Author claim:** The chapter describes SIMDAC and SHADO, field studies in architectural practices, iterative prototyping with designers, interviews and a “Plan of Action” that is always subject to evaluation and modification.
- **Evidence-supported claim:** Pages 8–9 say objectives are revisited as information arrives and that the models were derived from field studies rather than only from conceptual deduction.
- **Researcher inference:** Early DDR computing includes an empirical and recursive strand that complicates any lineage from systematic method directly to deterministic automation.
- **Evidence (quote/paraphrase + page):** Boyd Davis states that any plan of action is subject to evaluation and modification and that SIMDAC/SHADO were rooted in real-world field studies. [@BoydDavis2024DesignInterestingPhenomenon, pp. 8–9]
- **Warrant (my words):** A computational model built and revised against observation performs a different epistemic role from a prescriptive algorithm imposed before empirical study.
- **Boundary:** The chapter reconstructs the project from reports; the degree to which models actually changed practice or design outcomes requires further evidence.
- **Consequence:** The thesis should separate computational representation, empirical modelling and practical uptake.
- **Practice cross-check:** TODO (inspect Report 108 series for revisions to models following field observation).

## Claim 5
- **Claim (plain):** DDR used computational analysis to engage subjective design judgements rather than restricting computation to quantifiable engineering data.
- **Author claim:** Boyd Davis describes Mallen's use of repertory grid techniques with seventeen architecture students and analysis through multidimensional scaling, principal-components analysis and cluster analysis to represent volunteered subjective criteria.
- **Evidence-supported claim:** Pages 10–12 explicitly frame this as “capturing the subjective” and contrast Mallen's interest in creativity and cognition with a purely rigorous treatment of design.
- **Researcher inference:** The historical relationship between computation and design subjectivity is not simply one of quantification replacing judgement; computation was also used to model subjective structures.
- **Evidence (quote/paraphrase + page):** The chapter describes three analytical techniques used to reduce and interpret an n-dimensional set of user-volunteered criteria while minimising information loss. [@BoydDavis2024DesignInterestingPhenomenon, pp. 10–12]
- **Warrant (my words):** Formal analysis can be applied to subjective judgements without turning those judgements into objective truths; the modelling operation itself must remain visible.
- **Boundary:** The study population is small and specific, and Boyd Davis's chapter does not provide enough methodological detail to reassess the statistical validity of the original analysis.
- **Consequence:** This offers a historically grounded precursor for the thesis's own concern with dimensionality reduction, but not methodological authority for current UMAP practice.

## Claim 6
- **Claim (plain):** Mallen and colleagues expand the unit of design analysis from the individual designer to organisations and a wider “design coalition.”
- **Author claim:** The chapter emphasises social workings at both individual and organisational level and quotes the DDR account that all organisations contributing to a design process constitute a design coalition operating in a general social context.
- **Evidence-supported claim:** Pages 10–12 move from individual information processing to organisational models and explicitly position design activity inside a wider social context.
- **Researcher inference:** Relationship-centred archive analysis is historically appropriate to this strand of DDR work because the primary concepts themselves distribute agency across people, organisations and environments.
- **Evidence (quote/paraphrase + page):** Boyd Davis quotes the DDR formulation that the “design coalition” includes the organisations involved in a design process and operates within general social context. [@BoydDavis2024DesignInterestingPhenomenon, pp. 10–12]
- **Warrant (my words):** If design agency is distributed across a coalition, an author-centred history cannot adequately represent the process.
- **Boundary:** The concept identifies distributed participation but does not automatically establish equal influence or contribution among members.
- **Consequence:** The thesis should reconstruct relations while preserving differences among participation, authority, labour and authorship.

# Definitions / terms this changes

- **Descriptive model:** model grounded in empirical observation of what happens. [@BoydDavis2024DesignInterestingPhenomenon, p. 7]
- **Normative model:** conceptual model specifying what a decision-maker should do. [@BoydDavis2024DesignInterestingPhenomenon, p. 7]
- **Design coalition:** organisations and actors contributing to a design process within wider social context. [@BoydDavis2024DesignInterestingPhenomenon, pp. 10–12]
- **Information environment:** adaptive informational context linking people, systems and design activity; a recurring Mallen concern. [@BoydDavis2024DesignInterestingPhenomenon, pp. 6–7]
- **Capturing the subjective:** use of empirical and computational methods to represent subjective design judgements without reducing the research question to engineering variables. [@BoydDavis2024DesignInterestingPhenomenon, pp. 10–12]

# My response

The chapter is particularly valuable because it opens DDR laterally. Computing appears not as a side story or a straightforward march toward automation, but as a site where designers investigated organisations, information, subjective judgement, feedback and human–machine relations. At the same time, the chapter recovers actors such as Patterson whose contribution is poorly served by founder-centred histories. Its final source note is methodologically important: the reconstruction depends on uneven archival survivals and retrospective testimony, so the chapter itself demonstrates the kind of provenance-aware historical synthesis this thesis needs.

# Cross-source / cross-lens synthesis

Boyd Davis 2024 extends the lineage from Archer's systematic method into a more plural DDR computational culture. The descriptive/normative distinction and field-based modelling resonate with the revisions traced by Boyd Davis and Gristwood 2017, while Mallen's interest in recursive plans, social coalitions and subjective data gives empirical substance to the move away from fixed first-generation method described by Cross 1993. Buckley's critique sharpens the historiographic stakes: Patterson's trajectory shows how information labour may become structurally central yet historiographically secondary. For the thesis, these sources together justify a relationship-centred account in which people, methods, organisations and computational models are traced with explicit evidential status rather than folded into a single heroic narrative.
