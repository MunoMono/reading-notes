# Position and context

This chapter is a situated intervention into white/Anglo feminist generalisation, not a generic call for diversity. Its opening announces the political position from which Lugones writes (p. 65), assembles Black and women-of-colour voices on relational self-knowledge (pp. 65–67), then asks why white feminist theorists responded to the challenge of racial exclusion as a problem of saving theoretical generalisation rather than as a demand for changed relationships (pp. 68–72). The conclusion is a structural challenge to theory itself: if plurality does not inform the form of explanation, the people on whom it imposes its categories must perform contortions to become legible (pp. 73–75).

# The author's main move

The main move is a reversal of the 'problem of difference': it is not racialized women who pose an inconvenient exception to an otherwise adequate feminist universal, but the theorising relation itself that must change. Lugones argues the corrective is *interactive*, and racism, colonialism and imperialism should not be collapsed into an abstract word 'difference' (pp. 68–72). The DDR connection is a deliberately limited research inference: a departmental narrative that adds missing names while retaining a leadership-first explanatory grammar may leave the mechanism of marginalisation intact.

# My response and assessment

The chapter is unusually exact about the failure of a disclaimer. A theorist may concede positional limits yet leave the excluded interlocutor responsible for correcting a complete-looking universal account (pp. 68–69). This yields a demanding benchmark for doctoral work: reflexivity must affect analytic choices. The counterpressure is that a historical account cannot avoid generalisation entirely; some abstractions are necessary for explaining institutional relationships. The relevant distinction is whether they remain revisable when testimony and source records complicate them. Lugones's method is polemical, relational, and intentionally addressed: it is not a sampled empirical study of all white feminist theorists, and its argument about racism must be kept intact rather than transposed wholesale to occupational status at DDR.

**Additional stress test:** The reading makes it insufficient to insert women into a canon without examining its rules of selection. But this can also become an overly broad accusation: a sparse catalogue entry is not itself proof of deliberate exclusion, and a later interview can reveal a perspective without automatically defeating contemporary administrative evidence. The research task is to trace mechanisms—description, selection, authorship conventions, retrospective reputation—without presuming motive.
