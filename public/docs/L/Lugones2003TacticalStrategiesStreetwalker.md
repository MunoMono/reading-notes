# Position and context

Lugones begins by disputing the elevation of theoretical detachment over situated, embodied forms of knowledge (pp. 207–210), diagnoses the institutional fiction of autonomous agency (pp. 210–211), and troubles Michel de Certeau's opposition between strategy and tactics (pp. 211–215). She develops active subjectivity, relational intentionality, and 'hangouts' as spaces of communicating and negotiating resistant sense (pp. 215–223). The later sections follow how words, gestures and intentions travel among encounters, and how organisations that promise redress can draw people back into dominant institutional logics (pp. 224–230). The conclusion complicates rather than abandons the question of responsibility.

# The author's main move

The central argument is a reconfiguration, not an abolition, of intention. The autonomous intending subject is institutionally supported but mistakes that support for individual potency (pp. 210–211); resistant intentions are formed and carried intersubjectively between subjects and overlapping contexts (pp. 216–218). 'Active subjectivity' allows an account of activities treated as inconsequential by prevailing categories (pp. 215–219). 'Hangouts' are material, permeable and communicative practices, not romanticised informal chats (pp. 220–221). Her contrast between intermeshed oppression and categorical interlocking (pp. 222–224) guards against simply treating different oppressions as detachable variables.

# Definitions and benchmark

**Tactical strategy:** embodied, socially perceptive practice that contests a binary between strategic mastery and momentary tactics (pp. 207–209, 214–216).

**Active subjectivity:** a category for resistant meaningful activity that institutionally backed models of autonomous agency fail to recognize (pp. 210–211, 215–219).

**Hangouts:** permeable spaces in which collective meaning and resistant intention can form (pp. 209, 220–221).

**My benchmark:** Use the chapter where evidence allows a meaningful distinction among documented action, credited authorship, attributed intention and inferred contribution. Never turn missing attribution into positive proof of historical participation.

# Thesis integration and synthesis

Chapter 3 requires that plurality be built into theory; Chapter 4 develops relational attention to others' situated worlds; Chapter 7 exposes recognitions that project familiar categories upon unfamiliar people. Chapter 10 completes this argument by asking who is recognized as an intending, knowledgeable actor and what institutionally unsupported collective practices are overlooked. In the DDR thesis, it opens a critical question about whether archival organization and historiography privilege articulated programmes, formal authorship and named leadership over informal and relational knowledge production. # My response and assessment

This is the strongest direct challenge to a retrospective history dominated by named strategies and individual authors, but the temptation to apply it too swiftly to Archer is substantial. Archer's methodical articulation of design decisions cannot be assumed to deny informal thinking; both formal methodology and tacit collaboration may co-exist. Conversely, a sparse archival trace is not evidence that an unnamed woman worked on a particular project. The test is whether formal descriptions and attribution practices omit demonstrable exchanges, and whether these exchanges materially change interpretation. Lugones's stress on institutional power and domination should never be neutralised into a cheerful organisational collaboration model.

**Later-chapter stress test:** Lugones warns that emancipatory work can be translated into institutionally respected expertise and administrative modes that reproduce dominant categorisations (pp. 229–230). This is a sharper warning for the PhD instrument than a generic celebration of collective practice: designing a scholarly AI apparatus might itself privilege what is measurable, creditable and institutionally intelligible. Its standards for evidence are essential safeguards but also worthy of reflexive evaluation.
