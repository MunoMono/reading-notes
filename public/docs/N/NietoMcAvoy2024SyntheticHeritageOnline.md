# Position + moment (2–4 lines)

Nieto McAvoy and Kidd write across Digital Memory Studies, Critical Algorithm Studies and genealogy research. Their 2024 article examines MyHeritage's Deep Nostalgia and DeepStory through close critical reading of platform discourse, policies and synthetic outputs. Rather than asking only whether synthetic media are authentic or false, they examine how algorithmic remediation redistributes agency, produces new mnemonic experiences and embeds historical material within corporate and platform infrastructures.

**Canon assumptions to problematise / update for 2026 (1–2 lines):**  
Digital heritage cannot be understood simply as digitised access to an existing past. Generative systems participate in producing new representations and experiences of that past, making the distinction between source, remediation and synthetic output methodologically consequential.

# The author’s main move (1 sentence)

They try to theorise synthetic heritage by showing how genealogy platforms algorithmically remediate archival photographs into new mnemonic objects whose apparent personalisation is shaped by platform infrastructures, commercial interests and homogenising computational processes.

## Claim 1

- **Claim (plain):** Algorithmic remediation can create new memories and attachments rather than simply reproducing existing historical material.
- **Evidence (quote/paraphrase + page):** The authors describe Deep Nostalgia and DeepStory as forms of “remediated memory” and report that these algorithmic remediations can scaffold existing mnemonic attachments while also supporting new posthumous memories, such as an imagined memory of a deceased person's smile. `[@NietoMcAvoy2024SyntheticHeritageOnline, p. 11]`
- **Warrant (my words):** Once archival material is animated or otherwise transformed, the output acquires experiential qualities that were absent from the source. The representation can therefore participate in the construction of memory rather than merely transmit historical evidence.
- **So what for my thesis (a reusable sentence):** Computational activation of archival traces can generate new interpretative and mnemonic relations to the past, so synthetic outputs must remain distinguishable from the source material they remediate.
- **Practice cross-check:** Turin generated answers connect DDR traces into new readable relationships; citations and source previews are therefore needed to keep that interpretative synthesis visibly distinct from the archival record itself.

## Claim 2

- **Claim (plain):** Algorithmic personalisation can conceal substantial homogenisation and imported material from outside the historical source.
- **Evidence (quote/paraphrase + page):** The authors show that Deep Nostalgia animations are created by mapping historical photographs onto pre-recorded “driver” videos, meaning that gestures such as blinking, smiling and head movement originate from other bodies rather than the photographed subject. They describe the outputs as synthetic remediations produced through abstraction, blending and pre-scripted prompts, with a high degree of uniformity despite claims of uniqueness. `[@NietoMcAvoy2024SyntheticHeritageOnline, pp. 11–12]`
- **Warrant (my words):** A generated representation can appear highly specific to a historical subject while drawing important expressive features from generic computational templates. Personalisation therefore does not guarantee historical specificity.
- **So what for my thesis:** AI-mediated historical synthesis can appear bespoke to a particular archive while still importing generic patterns of language, relation and narrative coherence that do not originate in the evidence.
- **Practice cross-check:** Turin: generative synthesis must not turn semantic similarity or recurring model patterns into apparently specific historical relationships without passage-level evidential support.

## Claim 3

- **Claim (plain):** Synthetic heritage becomes ethically and epistemically safer when its synthetic status is made explicit, although signalling alone does not resolve the wider effects of remediation.
- **Evidence (quote/paraphrase + page):** MyHeritage explicitly states that its outputs are technological simulations rather than authentic representations and marks synthetic media with watermarks or symbols indicating AI generation. `[@NietoMcAvoy2024SyntheticHeritageOnline, p. 14]` The authors nevertheless argue that the ambiguity and deceptive qualities of these outputs remain ethically consequential because they operate within broader mnemonic, affective and platform dynamics. `[@NietoMcAvoy2024SyntheticHeritageOnline, pp. 14–15]`
- **Warrant (my words):** Disclosure establishes an important distinction between source and synthetic intervention but cannot by itself neutralise the authority, affect or interpretative effects of the generated object.
- **So what for my thesis:** Provenance signalling is necessary but insufficient: an archive-facing AI system should identify generated interpretation clearly while also allowing users to inspect the evidence and understand the limits of what that synthesis establishes.

# Definitions / terms this changes (only the ones that matter)

- **Synthetic heritage:** my use of the authors’ framing for historical or mnemonic representations created through algorithmic transformation of heritage material rather than direct reproduction of surviving evidence.
- **Remediated memory:** memory reshaped through mediation, here specifically through algorithmic processes that refashion existing photographs and other historical material into new mnemonic objects. `[@NietoMcAvoy2024SyntheticHeritageOnline, pp. 1, 11]`
- **Deceptive genealogy:** the authors’ term for synthetic genealogical representations whose constructed or deceptive status may be recognised but whose effects on memory, representation and historical connection remain consequential. `[@NietoMcAvoy2024SyntheticHeritageOnline, pp. 12–15]`
- **Algorithmic homogenisation:** the tendency for supposedly personalised outputs to converge around constrained templates, categories and behavioural patterns encoded within the system. `[@NietoMcAvoy2024SyntheticHeritageOnline, pp. 11–12]`
