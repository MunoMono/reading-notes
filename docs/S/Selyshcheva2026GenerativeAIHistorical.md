# Position + moment (2–4 lines)

Selyshcheva writes explicitly from historical methodology and digital history at the point at which generative AI has moved from experimental technology into everyday scholarly practice. The article synthesises peer-reviewed studies, professional guidance, legal cases and documented failures rather than presenting new empirical data. Its intervention is disciplinary: it asks how the historian's existing craft of source criticism should be extended to probabilistic generative systems.

**Canon assumptions to problematise / update for 2026 (1–2 lines):**  
Traditional source criticism assumes that the historian can identify and interrogate the provenance of an artefact. Generative systems complicate this because their training corpora are largely opaque, mutable and inaccessible, so source criticism must address both the generated statement and the computational infrastructure that produced it.

# The author’s main move (1 sentence)

She tries to bring generative AI within the methodological discipline of history by treating the model as an object of source criticism and making verification, provenance and human judgement conditions of its responsible scholarly use.

# Three-claim evidence ledger (max 3 claims)

## Claim 1

- **Claim (plain):** Fluent AI output can convert historical uncertainty into unwarranted certainty.
- **Evidence (quote/paraphrase + page):** Selyshcheva identifies specifically historical forms of generative failure: incorrect dates and chronology, omission of documented events, fabricated actors or actions, conflation of tentative hypotheses with established fact, and misattribution of figures or events across periods. She argues that these follow from systems optimised for plausible continuation rather than truth verification. `[@Selyshcheva2026GenerativeAIHistorical, p. 292]`
- **Warrant (my words):** Historical meaning depends on distinctions in attribution, chronology, modality and evidential status. When synthesis changes any of those relations, it changes the historical proposition rather than merely its wording.
- **So what for my thesis (a reusable sentence):** AI-mediated historical synthesis can transform evidential uncertainty into apparent historical certainty, making preservation of attribution, chronology, qualification and provenance a methodological requirement rather than a presentational preference.
- **Practice cross-check:** Turin bounded inference: retrieved DDR traces must not be converted from recollection into fact, association into influence, chronology into causation or unresolved attribution into named responsibility simply because a fluent synthesis can be produced.

## Claim 2

- **Claim (plain):** The biases and silences of digitised historical records can be reproduced through generative systems.
- **Evidence (quote/paraphrase + page):** Selyshcheva describes the LLM as an “algorithmic cartography” of the digitised record: an uneven and predominantly English-language, Global-North representation rather than a neutral sample of historical knowledge. She argues that examining where models succeed and fail can reveal which histories have been digitised and absorbed into AI infrastructures and which remain obscure. `[@Selyshcheva2026GenerativeAIHistorical, p. 290]` Later she connects generative systems directly to colonial silences and inherited representational biases in training data. `[@Selyshcheva2026GenerativeAIHistorical, pp. 293–294]`
- **Warrant (my words):** Generative output is conditioned by what has previously been digitised, described, indexed and made computationally available. AI therefore operates downstream of historical and archival selection rather than above it.
- **So what for my thesis:** Computational activation cannot repair archival partiality merely by increasing scale; retrieval and generation operate over an already uneven evidential landscape and can reproduce its patterns of visibility unless those limits are made explicit.
- **Practice cross-check:** Turin Semantic Atlas and scoped missingness: densely documented DDR actors may dominate semantic retrieval while less-described labour, students or marginal voices remain difficult to surface; absence and representational density therefore require explicit examination rather than being treated as neutral properties of the corpus.

## Claim 3

- **Claim (plain):** Historical use of generative AI requires independently verifiable evidence and continued human interpretative authority.
- **Evidence (quote/paraphrase + page):** Selyshcheva describes verification of every generated reference as a “non-negotiable” condition of scholarly use following documented cases of fabricated citations. `[@Selyshcheva2026GenerativeAIHistorical, pp. 292–293]` Her conclusion argues for a deliberate partnership between computational capability and human judgement and retains causal interpretation, argument construction, historical empathy and interpretation of archival silences as responsibilities of historians. `[@Selyshcheva2026GenerativeAIHistorical, pp. 295–296]`
- **Warrant (my words):** Computational systems can accelerate retrieval, transcription and structural analysis while lacking independent authority to determine what the historical record warrants. Verification and interpretation therefore remain separate operations.
- **So what for my thesis:** Retrieval-augmented inference should support historical judgement by exposing evidence and relationships while retaining the researcher as the agent who determines whether those relationships are historically warranted.
- **Practice cross-check:** Turin citation architecture: generated statements link back to specific source passages; scoped missingness prevents unsupported completion; the researcher retains responsibility for accepting, qualifying or rejecting the resulting interpretation.

# Definitions / terms this changes (only the ones that matter)

- **Algorithmic cartography:** Selyshcheva's metaphor for the LLM as an uneven statistical representation of the digitised record whose capabilities and failures reflect the historical materials incorporated into contemporary computational infrastructures. `[@Selyshcheva2026GenerativeAIHistorical, p. 290]`
- **Jagged frontier:** an irregular boundary between tasks on which AI performs strongly and those on which it fails, with the additional warning that factual fabrication can occur even inside apparent zones of high competence. `[@Selyshcheva2026GenerativeAIHistorical, p. 290]`
- **Source criticism of the model:** interrogation of the conditions, corpus, biases and mechanisms through which an AI output is produced rather than treating generated text as neutral information. `[@Selyshcheva2026GenerativeAIHistorical, pp. 290, 295]`
- **Citation integrity:** the requirement that every generated scholarly reference be independently verified against an identifiable original source. `[@Selyshcheva2026GenerativeAIHistorical, pp. 292–293]`
- **Source-critical AI mediation:** my formulation for the DDR method: generated interpretation remains an explicitly mediated scholarly object whose claims must be traceable to the archive and subjected to the historian's evidential judgement.
