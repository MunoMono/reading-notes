## Claim 2
- **Claim (plain):** The chapter argues that Mallen’s distinctive contribution lay in modelling subjective, social, and organisational aspects of design rather than only formal or prescriptive procedure.
- **Evidence (quote/paraphrase + page):** Boyd Davis shows Mallen distinguishing descriptive models from normative models in the architectural design studies, emphasising empirical observation of real practice. He highlights Mallen’s use of repertory-grid-derived techniques and multidimensional analysis to capture subjective characteristics of design problems, and quotes Mallen saying he never believed design could be treated rigorously in Archer’s way because he was more interested in creativity and “the subjective aspects of design.” The chapter also stresses that Mallen’s work treated drawings, teams, organisations, and the wider “design coalition” as central. `[@BoydDavis2024DesignInterestingPhenomenon, pp. 7–12]`
- **Warrant (my words):** This evidence supports the claim because Boyd Davis repeatedly positions Mallen’s work against prescriptive or purely formal models, showing instead a concern with empirical fieldwork, subjective criteria, organisational life, and human-computer relations.
- **So what for my thesis:** This chapter gives me a strong route for linking DDR to later concerns with situated practice, subjectivity, and interaction, and for showing that computational work in DDR was not reducible to formalism or optimisation.
