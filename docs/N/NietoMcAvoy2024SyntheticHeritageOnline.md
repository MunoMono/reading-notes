# Thesis job

**How this source moves the primary research question forward:** Nieto McAvoy and Kidd show that generative systems do not merely expose historical materials; they remediate them into new synthetic representations that acquire affective, mnemonic and interpretative force. This gives the thesis a conceptual basis for distinguishing archival trace from AI-mediated synthesis.

**How this source bears on the secondary question:** Revisiting historical ideas through generative systems creates new representations of the past. Those representations can be useful, but they also import platform logics, generic patterns and new forms of authority that must remain visible.

**Why I’m reading this now:** It is the strongest bridge text for the thesis’s “synthetic heritage” concept and the distinction between source, computational transformation and generated interpretation.

**Where it sits in my argument:** Contemporary bridge literature connecting critical algorithm studies, digital memory and heritage synthesis.

# Position + moment

Nieto McAvoy and Kidd analyse MyHeritage’s Deep Nostalgia and DeepStory through digital memory studies and critical algorithm studies. Their object is not merely deepfake authenticity but the wider socio-technical process through which genealogy platforms transform historical photographs into synthetic mnemonic objects and circulate them through commercial, networked infrastructures. [@NietoMcAvoy2024SyntheticHeritageOnline, pp. 1–5]

# The author’s main move

The authors argue that synthetic genealogy redistributes mnemonic agency among users, platforms, algorithms, source photographs and generic computational “drivers”; the result can feel personalised while being shaped by homogenising templates, commercial platform logics and ethically consequential forms of simulation. [@NietoMcAvoy2024SyntheticHeritageOnline, pp. 1–15]

# Definitions / terms this changes

- **Synthetic heritage:** historical or mnemonic representations produced through algorithmic transformation rather than direct reproduction of surviving evidence.
- **Remediated memory:** memory reshaped through media processes, here specifically algorithmic transformations that refashion historical materials into new mnemonic objects. [@NietoMcAvoy2024SyntheticHeritageOnline, pp. 1, 11]
- **Deceptive genealogy:** synthetic genealogical representation whose constructed quality may be acknowledged yet remains consequential for memory and representation. [@NietoMcAvoy2024SyntheticHeritageOnline, pp. 12–15]
- **Algorithmic homogenisation:** convergence of supposedly personalised outputs around constrained templates and categories. [@NietoMcAvoy2024SyntheticHeritageOnline, pp. 11–12]
- **Platform mnemotechnology:** the platform as a socio-technical infrastructure that produces, circulates and monetises technologically mediated memory. [@NietoMcAvoy2024SyntheticHeritageOnline, pp. 6–15]

# My response

Nieto McAvoy and Kidd provide the strongest conceptual warning against treating computational activation as transparent access to the past. The synthetic object is new: it may be source-informed, but it is produced through transformations, templates and institutional infrastructures that add meanings and affordances of their own. For DDR, this supports a strict distinction between archival trace, retrieved evidence and generated interpretation without requiring a retreat from generative methods altogether.

# Cross-source / cross-lens synthesis

Nieto McAvoy and Kidd give the computational framework its cultural-memory account of synthesis. Zaagsma shows that digitisation and search already mediate the evidence surface; Bender et al. show that model outputs reproduce statistical patterns rather than understanding; Asai et al. show how retrieval can improve evidence-grounded synthesis; Bernard and Balog show how ranking controls visibility; Schwartz and Cook show that every archival layer participates in memory-making. The resulting DDR position is that RAI is neither neutral retrieval nor free-standing generation: it is a bounded synthetic intervention whose source chain, transformations and evidential limits must remain inspectable.
