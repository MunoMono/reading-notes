# Position + moment (2–4 lines)
Archer writes in the inaugural issue of *Design Studies* in 1979, from his institutional position at the RCA as Professor of Design Research and head of major theoretical and educational units. The piece is both retrospective and programmatic: he looks back on two and a half decades of design methods work, acknowledges what failed to connect with practising designers, and relocates the surviving project within the wider field of design research. `[@ArcherWhateverBecameDesign, pp. 1–2]`  
**Canon assumptions to problematise / update for 2026 (1–2 lines):** Archer is often reduced either to a hero or villain of rationalised design methods. This text supports a more precise account: by 1979 he is already revising that work, criticising its alien languages and directional models, and arguing for designerly cognition and modelling as distinct forms of enquiry. `[@ArcherWhateverBecameDesign, pp. 1–2]`

# The author’s main move (1 sentence)
They try to rescue the intellectual project behind design methodology by re-situating it within design research and by redefining design activity as an ill-defined, commutative, designerly mode of thinking in order to preserve rigour without reducing design to alien procedural models.

## Claim 1
- **Claim (plain):** Archer says design methodology did not disappear; it was absorbed and broadened under the name of design research.
- **Evidence (quote/paraphrase + page):** The article opens with the blunt statement: “Design methodology is alive and well, and living under the name of Design research.” He then says he never liked the term “design methodology” because it implied exclusive concern with procedure, whereas his own motive was “ends-directed, not means-oriented.” `[@ArcherWhateverBecameDesign, p. 1]`
- **Warrant (my words):** This is the key retrospective correction. Archer is telling readers that the project did not end when enthusiasm for “methodology” cooled; instead, it was renamed and conceptually widened.

## Claim 2
- **Claim (plain):** Archer explicitly rejects the idea that his methods work was about downgrading qualitative judgement; his aim was to make qualitative concerns more defensible.
- **Evidence (quote/paraphrase + page):** Archer states that his concern was to find ways of ensuring that “comfort and convenience, ethics and beauty” should be “as carefully taken into account and as doggedly defensible under attack” as “strength, cost and durability.” He adds that even quantitative considerations rest on assumptions that are never wholly value-free. `[@ArcherWhateverBecameDesign, p. 1]`
- **Warrant (my words):** This matters because it revises the usual caricature of early methods as a purely technocratic assault on judgement. Archer is presenting his own project as an attempt to formalise accountability around qualitative as well as quantitative criteria.
- **So what for my thesis:** This essay supports a reading of DDR as an attempt to make contested values in design explicit and discussable, rather than to replace them with neutral calculation.

## Claim 3
- **Claim (plain):** Archer argues that design problems are ill-defined and that design works through a distinctive, oscillating process best understood through modelling rather than linear procedure or alien formal languages.
- **Evidence (quote/paraphrase + page):** Archer says design problems are “ill-defined,” because requirements do not contain enough information for solution by transformation or optimisation alone; some information is unknowable, variable, or incompatible. He redefines “the problem” as obscurity and misfit between requirements and provisions, and “the solution” as an acceptable requirement/provision match. Design activity is therefore “commutative,” with attention oscillating between requirement ideas and provision ideas. He adds that early methods theories alienated designers through “directionality and causality and separation of analysis from synthesis,” and that design operates through a medium called “modelling,” distinct from words, mathematics, or scientific notation. `[@ArcherWhateverBecameDesign, pp. 1–2]`
- **Warrant (my words):** This is Archer’s reflective reformulation of design method. He is not abandoning structure altogether, but he is clearly moving away from linear process models and toward a theory of designerly cognition and modelling.
- **So what for my thesis:** Archer’s retrospective account helps me frame DDR as internally self-revising: the early procedural models give way to a stronger emphasis on ill-defined problems, oscillation, modelling, and design-specific epistemology.

# Definitions / terms this changes (only the ones that matter)
- **Design research:** the broader field within which design methodology survives, alongside design history, philosophy, criticism, epistemology, modelling, measurement, management, and education; I will use this as Archer’s own retrospective umbrella term. `[@ArcherWhateverBecameDesign, p. 2]`
- **Design methodology:** a term Archer treats as too procedure-centred and too narrow for the ends-directed project he thought he was pursuing. `[@ArcherWhateverBecameDesign, p. 1]`
- **Ill-defined problem:** a problem in which requirements do not contain sufficient information for resolution by analysis or optimisation alone, because crucial information may be unknown, unstable, incompatible, or unknowable. `[@ArcherWhateverBecameDesign, p. 1]`
- **Commutative design activity:** Archer’s account of design as oscillation between requirement ideas and provision ideas rather than a one-way sequence from analysis to synthesis. `[@ArcherWhateverBecameDesign, pp. 1–2]`
- **Modelling:** the distinct medium through which design thinking operates, comparable with but different from language and notation; I will use this as a key bridge to later S3 visual work. `[@ArcherWhateverBecameDesign, p. 2]`
