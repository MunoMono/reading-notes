## Claim 1
- **Claim (plain):** Jonas argues that design research cannot be reduced to scientific knowledge production because design deals with unstable, value-laden fits in changing contexts.
- **Evidence (quote/paraphrase + page):** Early in the essay he contrasts science and design: science aims at predictability and stable models that repeatedly deliver “the same,” while design aims at single new phenomena that must fit unforeseeable conditions. He says design knowledge cannot be called simply “true” or “false” in the same way, because design environments change too fast, and describes the archive of design knowledge as “a memory, a growing reservoir of variations as well as restrictions.” He adds that design expertise involves dealing with scientific and non-scientific knowledge, fuzzy and outdated knowledge, and even no knowledge at all. `[@Jonas2012ExploringSwampyGround, pp. 2–3]`
- **Warrant (my words):** This claim matters because Jonas is not anti-rigour; he is shifting the standard of rigour away from stable prediction and toward situated fitting, variation, and value-laden judgement. That makes design research structurally different from a simple import of scientific method.
- **So what for my thesis (a reusable sentence):** Jonas helps me frame the DDR archive as a reservoir of design knowledge whose value lies not in timeless truth claims but in accumulated variations, restrictions, and situated attempts to achieve fit.

## Claim 2
- **Claim (plain):** Jonas reads the history of design research as an unfinished struggle over how to balance scientific method, designerly knowledge, and reflective practice.
- **Evidence (quote/paraphrase + page):** In his historical mapping he revisits Simon, Archer, Cross, Rittel, Findeli, Frayling and Friedman. He notes Cross’s warning that design research should avoid being swamped by imported cultures from science or art, but replies that “we are in the swamp already, necessarily.” He uses Schön’s “swampy lowland” to insist that the most important problems are messy and cannot be treated as narrowly technical. He then criticises Friedman’s strong separation between reflection and research as “a step backward” compared with the more plural and hybrid models of design research. `[@Jonas2012ExploringSwampyGround, pp. 4–5, 8]`
- **Warrant (my words):** This is the paper’s historical-critical core. Jonas is showing that the field has repeatedly tried to purify itself—toward science, toward discipline, toward classification—but that its real condition is hybrid, reflexive, and messy.
- **So what for my thesis:** Jonas gives me a strong way to write the DDR legacy as contested and recursively reinterpreted, rather than as a clean progression from methods to post-methods.

## Claim 3
- **Claim (plain):** Jonas’s positive proposal is that research through design integrates other forms of inquiry and is best understood through cybernetic models of phases and observer positions.
- **Evidence (quote/paraphrase + page):** Jonas argues that hybrid and integrative models are emerging and proposes a generic triadic process of **analysis – projection – synthesis** across earlier authors, shown in Table 9 on page 11. He says the scientific paradigm must be embedded in the design paradigm: design research is guided by design process logic, supported by phases of scientific inquiry, and must not reduce complexity unduly. On pages 12–14 he then develops a second-order cybernetic matrix of observer positions that differentiates research **FOR**, **ABOUT**, and **THROUGH** design, plus an “inaccessible” **AS** category linked to abduction and new idea generation; the diagram on page 13 and the summary table on page 14 make this explicit. `[@Jonas2012ExploringSwampyGround, pp. 11–14]`
- **Warrant (my words):** This is where Jonas moves from critique to method. He is arguing that design research becomes coherent not by imitating science but by explicitly orchestrating different observer positions and kinds of knowledge around the design process itself.
- **So what for my thesis:** Jonas helps me articulate archive activation as a hybrid research process: historical and interpretative work can be research about design, while computational and visual reactivation may become research through design when it generates transferable knowledge rather than only artefacts.

# Definitions / terms this changes (only the ones that matter)
- **Swampy ground:** the messy lowland of practice where the most important human problems sit and where technical-rational solutions are insufficient; I will use it as a shorthand for the irreducible complexity of design research situations. `[@Jonas2012ExploringSwampyGround, pp. 4–5]`
- **Research THROUGH design:** the reflected and purposive use of the design process itself to generate transferable knowledge and innovation; I will use this carefully for computational or practice-based activation work that does more than produce an artefact. `[@Jonas2012ExploringSwampyGround, pp. 6–8, 11–14]`
- **Analysis / Projection / Synthesis:** Jonas’s triadic methodological pattern for design research, where projection is the abductive bridge that integrates science and design. `[@Jonas2012ExploringSwampyGround, pp. 11–12]`
- **Projection:** the abductive phase in which new concepts are generated and the gap between analysis and synthesis is temporarily bridged; I will use this term where the thesis discusses conceptual or visual modelling as a knowledge-producing move. `[@Jonas2012ExploringSwampyGround, p. 12]`
- **Research FOR / ABOUT / THROUGH design:** observer-positioned categories that distinguish normative support for design, disciplinary inquiry into design, and embedded knowledge generation through designing; I will use these as analytic distinctions rather than fixed ontological boxes. `[@Jonas2012ExploringSwampyGround, pp. 13–14]`
