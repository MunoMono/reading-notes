# Position + moment

Jaillant, Aske and Caputo write from digital humanities, archival practice and computer science, using The National Archives (UK) to examine born-digital scale, sensitivity review, discovery, authenticity, explainability and trust in cultural-heritage AI. # The author’s main move

They use TNA projects to show that computational methods can help cultural-heritage organisations manage born-digital scale only when access, risk, provenance, uncertainty and institutional accountability remain part of the workflow. [@JaillantNavigatingArtificialIntelligence, pp. 15–39]

# Definitions / terms this changes

- **Digital recordkeeping at scale:**  
  I will use this to describe the problem of managing archival material whose volume, format diversity and dispersal exceed manual paper-era workflows. The phrase helps explain why selection, review and discovery become methodological problems, not just technical tasks [@JaillantNavigatingArtificialIntelligence, p. 16].

- **Sensitivity review:**  
  I will use this as a reminder that making archival material accessible is not the same as making it public without constraint. The chapter frames sensitivity review as a technology-assisted but human-overseen process, especially where personal or exempt information may be present [@JaillantNavigatingArtificialIntelligence, p. 19].

- **Topic modelling:**  
  I will use this as one possible form of computational surfacing. It can show latent patterns across catalogue descriptions or text corpora, but it requires interpretation and cannot substitute for close archival reading [@JaillantNavigatingArtificialIntelligence, p. 20].

- **Explainable AI:**  
  I will use this carefully. In this source, explainability is not just a technical feature. It is part of a trust relationship between users, tools, institutions and contexts [@JaillantNavigatingArtificialIntelligence, pp. 34–35].

- **Risk appetite:**  
  I will use this as an institutional term for the level of uncertainty an archive is prepared to accept when balancing access, privacy, legal responsibility and public value [@JaillantNavigatingArtificialIntelligence, p. 19].

# Cross-source / cross-lens synthesis

Jaillant, Aske and Caputo supply the institutional bridge between critical archival theory and the computational strand. Their emphasis on selection, discovery, sensitivity, provenance and human oversight aligns with Zaagsma’s politics of digitisation and with Bearman’s insistence that information systems preserve archival context. Read alongside Asai, Wang and Zhu, the key limit becomes clear: retrieval and model-assisted synthesis may improve access to dispersed traces, but computationally surfaced proximity or coherence does not itself establish historical relationship. The DDR method therefore needs both computational activation and explicit evidential controls.
