# Position + moment (2–4 lines)
Caswell, Punzalan, and Sangwand write in 2017 at a moment when archival studies had already absorbed substantial work on power, human rights, community archives, decoloniality, feminism, queer theory, and critical race theory. Their introduction is programmatic: it names this constellation as “critical archival studies” and argues that such work should be understood as a coherent, legitimised field of inquiry. `[@Caswell2017CriticalArchivalStudies, pp. 1–4]`
**Canon assumptions to problematise / update for 2026 (1–2 lines):** This text is most useful when treated as a field-framing intervention, not as a neutral survey. Its key move is to turn critical archival work into an explicit agenda for transforming both archival practice and wider social relations. `[@Caswell2017CriticalArchivalStudies, pp. 2–4, 6]`

# The author’s main move (1 sentence)
They try to name and define critical archival studies as an emancipatory field by drawing on critical theory and recent archival scholarship in order to legitimise archival work that explains injustice, proposes practical change, and provides norms for critique.

## Claim 1
- **Claim (plain):** The authors define critical archival studies as a field that must explain injustice, propose practical change, and provide norms for critique.
- **Evidence (quote/paraphrase + page):** Drawing on Horkheimer, they broadly define critical archival studies as approaches that “(1) explain what is unjust with the current state of archival research and practice, (2) posit practical goals for how such research and practice can and should change, and/or (3) provide the norms for such critique.” They add that critical archival studies is “emancipatory in nature,” with the ultimate goal of transforming archival practice and society more broadly. `[@Caswell2017CriticalArchivalStudies, p. 2]`

## Claim 2
- **Claim (plain):** The authors argue that archives are bound up with oppression and liberation, and that there is no neutral archival position outside power.
- **Evidence (quote/paraphrase + page):** They say recent scholarship has examined “the ways in which records and archives serve as tools for both oppression and liberation,” including bringing about or impeding social justice, permitting continued silences, and empowering historically or contemporarily marginalized communities. They then write that “power permeates every aspect of the archival endeavor,” quoting Harris on the archive as political possibility, and state explicitly that “there is no neutral in archives.” `[@Caswell2017CriticalArchivalStudies, pp. 1–3]`
- **Warrant (my words):** This sharpens the implications of Schwartz and Cook. The point is not only that archives are implicated in power, but that archival action is always already positioned within struggles over justice, silence, and community voice.
- **So what for my thesis:** This essay supports a reading of the DDR archive as a site where design knowledge is mediated through power-laden archival choices, and where silences or marginalisations must be analysed as active conditions rather than accidental gaps.

## Claim 3
- **Claim (plain):** The authors argue that archival studies should not merely apply outside critical theories, but should also disrupt how the humanities think about evidence, knowledge, and transmission.
- **Evidence (quote/paraphrase + page):** They write that the contribution of archival studies is not simply to “apply” feminist theory, queer theory, or critical race theory to archives, but to “disrupt the ontological and epistemological assumptions of the humanities.” They argue that archival studies can trouble assumptions about record, provenance, value, and representation, taking “a long view of how potential evidence gets transmitted across space and time.” `[@Caswell2017CriticalArchivalStudies, pp. 3–4]`
- **Warrant (my words):** This is especially useful for your project because it makes archives methodologically generative, not just objects of critique. Archival thinking changes how we understand evidence itself.

# Definitions / terms this changes (only the ones that matter)
- **Critical archival studies:** an emancipatory approach to archives that explains injustice, proposes practical change, and provides norms for critique; I will use this as a field label for justice-oriented archival interpretation. `[@Caswell2017CriticalArchivalStudies, p. 2]`
- **Emancipatory:** oriented toward transforming archival practice and society rather than merely describing them; I will use this term cautiously to mark work that has a change-oriented purpose. `[@Caswell2017CriticalArchivalStudies, p. 2]`
- **No neutral in archives:** the principle that archival action is always implicated in power; I will use this as a shorthand warning against treating the DDR archive as a neutral container. `[@Caswell2017CriticalArchivalStudies, p. 3]`
- **Critical archival praxis:** the integration of theory and practice in ways that confront oppression and enable liberatory possibilities; I will use this where archive interpretation implies practical consequences for description, access, or representation. `[@Caswell2017CriticalArchivalStudies, pp. 4–6]`
