# Position + moment

Caswell, Punzalan and Sangwand write in 2017 to introduce a special issue on critical archival studies. They position the field as building on earlier work on archives and power while drawing on decolonial, feminist, queer, critical-race and postcolonial approaches. [@Caswell2017CriticalArchivalStudies, pp. 1–2]

# The author’s main move

They define critical archival studies as emancipatory archival scholarship and practice that explains injustice, proposes practical goals for change and provides norms for critique, while also arguing that archival studies can reshape how the humanities understand evidence and knowledge. [@Caswell2017CriticalArchivalStudies, pp. 2–6]

## Claim 2
- **Claim (plain):** Archives can support oppression and liberation.
- **Author claim:** The authors situate recent archival scholarship around the role of records in social justice, past wrongs, silence, human rights and community empowerment.
- **Evidence-supported claim:** Page 1 explicitly describes archives and records as tools for both oppression and liberation.
- **Researcher inference:** The DDR archive cannot be characterised simply as oppressive or emancipatory; its effects depend on how records were created, selected, described and later mobilised.
- **Evidence (quote/paraphrase + page):** The introduction lists research on silences, marginalised communities, oppressive regimes and human-rights evidence. [@Caswell2017CriticalArchivalStudies, p. 1]
- **Warrant (my words):** Archival infrastructures can simultaneously preserve evidence and reproduce exclusions.
- **Boundary:** The paper is field-defining, not a case study demonstrating the balance of these effects in any one archive.
- **Consequence:** The thesis should specify where DDR archival structures constrain or enable particular historical questions.
- **Practice cross-check:** Compare richly described canonical DDR actors with sparsely described support, student and women's labour.

## Claim 3
- **Claim (plain):** There is no neutral position in archival work.
- **Author claim:** The authors argue that power permeates record creation, archival functions, institutions, outreach, professional identity and core concepts.
- **Evidence-supported claim:** Page 3 explicitly states that “there is no neutral in archives.”
- **Researcher inference:** Choices in corpus construction, metadata normalisation, retrieval and interface ranking must be treated as interpretative archival actions rather than neutral engineering.
- **Evidence (quote/paraphrase + page):** The authors enumerate where power operates across the archival endeavour and connect critique to race, gender, class, sexuality and ability. [@Caswell2017CriticalArchivalStudies, p. 3]
- **Warrant (my words):** Selection and organisation distribute what becomes easier or harder to know.
- **Boundary:** Non-neutrality does not imply that evidence standards are impossible or that all decisions are equally arbitrary.
- **Consequence:** The thesis should document technical and curatorial decisions and their likely consequences for visibility.
- **Practice cross-check:** Preserve corpus boundary, retrieval settings and provenance receipts in the computational method.

## Claim 4
- **Claim (plain):** Naming a field is itself an exercise of power.
- **Author claim:** The authors note that critical archival work predates the label but argue that naming “critical archival studies” legitimates and demarcates a field.
- **Evidence-supported claim:** Page 3 states that naming is a form of legitimating, defining and harnessing.
- **Researcher inference:** The thesis's own categories — “testamentary traces,” “scoped missingness,” “synthetic heritage” — should be presented as analytic constructions with declared purposes, not natural archival kinds.
- **Evidence (quote/paraphrase + page):** The authors make the politics of naming explicit when introducing the field. [@Caswell2017CriticalArchivalStudies, p. 3]
- **Warrant (my words):** Categories structure attention and can consolidate some phenomena while obscuring alternatives.
- **Boundary:** Analytical naming can still be useful when its scope and consequences are made explicit.
- **Consequence:** New thesis terms should carry definitions, boundaries and evidential tests.
- **Practice cross-check:** Maintain glossary definitions and avoid using framework categories as evidence of historical actors' own terminology.

## Claim 5
- **Claim (plain):** Archival studies can disrupt the humanities' assumptions about evidence and knowledge.
- **Author claim:** The authors argue that archival studies should not merely apply external critical theory but challenge ontological and epistemological assumptions about records, provenance, value and representation.
- **Evidence-supported claim:** Page 4 calls for a “long view” of how potential evidence is transmitted across space and time.
- **Researcher inference:** The DDR archive is not simply a container supplying facts to design history; its modes of transmission are part of the historical argument.
- **Evidence (quote/paraphrase + page):** The article says archival studies can trouble how the humanities understand how knowledge is known and transmitted. [@Caswell2017CriticalArchivalStudies, p. 4]
- **Warrant (my words):** Evidence becomes historically meaningful only through chains of creation, preservation, description and access.
- **Boundary:** This does not remove the need for subject-specific historical expertise.
- **Consequence:** The thesis can make archival mediation part of design historiography rather than confining it to methods.
- **Practice cross-check:** Show how DDR catalogue structures and digitisation boundaries affect the relationships recoverable from the corpus.

## Claim 6
- **Claim (plain):** Critical archival theory is inseparable from praxis.
- **Author claim:** The issue's examples emphasise participatory, post-custodial, feminist, queer and anti-racist interventions and reject a theory/practice binary.
- **Evidence-supported claim:** Pages 4–6 repeatedly frame critique as action capable of redistributing archival power and reorienting institutions.
- **Researcher inference:** The thesis's practical archive interventions should be treated as part of the theoretical argument when they operationalise critique in interface, provenance or retrieval design.
- **Evidence (quote/paraphrase + page):** The conclusion calls on archival experts to identify injustices, figure out how to change them and enact those changes. [@Caswell2017CriticalArchivalStudies, pp. 4–6]
- **Warrant (my words):** A critical framework gains methodological force when its claims change how archival systems are built or used.
- **Boundary:** Institutional transformation at the scale discussed in the issue exceeds what one research prototype can demonstrate.
- **Consequence:** The thesis should make modest, testable claims about its own interventions rather than equating a prototype with systemic reform.
- **Practice cross-check:** Link scoped missingness, source-status separation and retrieval transparency to the archival problems each is designed to address.

# Definitions / terms this changes

- **Critical archival studies:** archival research/practice that explains injustice, proposes attainable change and/or establishes norms for critique. [@Caswell2017CriticalArchivalStudies, p. 2]
- **Archival power:** power operating across record creation, archival functions, institutions, use and the profession itself. [@Caswell2017CriticalArchivalStudies, p. 3]
- **Archival praxis:** theoretically informed action that changes archival relationships and practices rather than leaving critique abstract. [@Caswell2017CriticalArchivalStudies, pp. 4–6]

# My response

This short text gives the theoretical framework an important discipline: it makes “critical” operational. The key test is whether a critique identifies a concrete injustice or limitation, explains how it is produced and changes practice accordingly. That is useful for DDR because claims about omission, bias or marginalisation can otherwise become generic. The article also authorises a stronger thesis move: archival mediation is not background infrastructure to design history; it changes what can count as evidence and therefore belongs inside the historiographic argument.

# Cross-source / cross-lens synthesis

Caswell, Punzalan and Sangwand consolidate the critical turn that Schwartz and Cook place around archival power and that Ketelaar develops through activation and memory. Bearman and Duranti show that description and documentation themselves embody historically specific choices about context and access. Together these texts make the archive an active knowledge infrastructure. For DDR, that means a critical framework must examine not only missing records but also the descriptive, technical and institutional processes through which some relationships become legible and others do not.
