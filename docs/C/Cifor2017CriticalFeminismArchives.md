# Position + moment

Cifor and Wood write from critical archival studies, feminist theory and social-justice-oriented archival scholarship. Their article appears in a 2017 special issue on critical archival studies and intervenes in debates about archives, power, representation, community archives and the archival turn.

The article is deliberately reflexive. It uses feminist historiography to examine how feminist praxis has shaped archival theory and practice, while also arguing that archival studies has often reduced feminist intervention to representation and collection development.

**Canon assumptions to problematise / update for 2026:**  
The article challenges any archival method that treats inclusion as sufficient. For my project, it helps update the move from “make DDR traces visible” to “ask how systems of description, access and institutional organisation shape what visibility can mean.”

# The author’s main move

Cifor and Wood use feminist historiography and critical archival studies to argue that feminist archival praxis must move beyond representational repair toward a deeper transformation of archival institutions, concepts and practices.

## Claim 1

- **Claim:**  
  Feminist archival politics cannot stop at better representation in collections.

- **Evidence:**  
  Cifor and Wood argue that archival theory and practice have not fully engaged with a feminist praxis aimed at more than attaining better representation of women in archives [@Cifor2017CriticalFeminismArchives, p. 1]. In the introduction, they state that many feminist interventions have focused on representation and collection development, but that critical feminism requires radical transformation beyond representational politics [@Cifor2017CriticalFeminismArchives, p. 2].

- **Warrant:**  
  This supports the claim because the authors distinguish between adding marginalised materials to existing archival systems and questioning the systems themselves. Representation matters, but it can leave intact the classificatory, institutional and professional structures that produced exclusion in the first place.

- **So what for my thesis:**  
  DDR activation should not be written simply as recovery or increased visibility; it should also ask how the archive’s categories, descriptions and institutional arrangements have shaped what can be found and what remains difficult to know.

## Claim 2

- **Claim:**  
  Archives are active sites of self-representation, self-historicisation and political formation.

- **Evidence:**  
  Cifor and Wood trace feminist and queer archival movements as techniques of self-representation that resisted the absence of minoritised populations from the historical record [@Cifor2017CriticalFeminismArchives, pp. 3–7]. They argue that these archival impulses challenged traditional expectations of the archive as a neutral or passive repository and instead identified the archive as an institution that shapes the historical record through exclusion and silence [@Cifor2017CriticalFeminismArchives, p. 5].

- **Warrant:**  
  This matters because archives do not merely preserve a pre-existing history. They participate in making collective identity, political continuity and historical intelligibility possible. Archival practice can therefore be both evidential and world-making.

- **So what for my thesis:**  
  The RCA’s DDR archive should be approached not only as a record of design research, but as a structure through which design research can be made historically legible, contested and reactivated.

## Claim 3

- **Claim:**  
  Archival concepts such as community, organisation and professional neutrality require critique.

- **Evidence:**  
  Cifor and Wood argue that critical feminist theory can contribute to archival discourse by critiquing concepts that have remained unquestioned, including community and organisation [@Cifor2017CriticalFeminismArchives, pp. 1, 3]. In their discussion of archival studies, they argue that feminist theory can challenge hierarchical archival organisations and question bureaucratic structures that routinise social relations and subordinate workers [@Cifor2017CriticalFeminismArchives, pp. 18–19]. They also warn that community archives, while valuable, can reproduce hierarchies and exclusions through their processes and interpretations of records [@Cifor2017CriticalFeminismArchives, pp. 19–21].

- **Warrant:**  
  This supports a more demanding ethics of archival activation. It is not enough to say that a project serves a “community”, improves “access” or preserves “memory”. Those terms can hide internal exclusions, institutional authority and uneven labour.

- **So what for my thesis:**  
  When I describe DDR activation, I need to specify whose access, whose interpretation, whose authority and whose labour are involved in making the archive more legible.

# Definitions / terms this changes

- **Critical feminism:**  
  I will use this as an intersectional political and methodological stance that seeks structural transformation, not only better representation within existing archival systems [@Cifor2017CriticalFeminismArchives, p. 2].

- **Feminist historiography:**  
  I will use this to mean a reflexive way of writing history that returns attention to evidence, power, politics, positionality and the researcher’s own process [@Cifor2017CriticalFeminismArchives, p. 3].

- **Self-representation / self-historicisation:**  
  I will use these terms for archival practices through which groups document themselves, produce continuity and resist absence or misrepresentation [@Cifor2017CriticalFeminismArchives, pp. 3–7].

- **Archival impulse:**  
  A political and practical drive to collect, preserve and organise records as part of social movement work, identity formation and future memory [@Cifor2017CriticalFeminismArchives, pp. 3–7].

- **Archival turn:**  
  A humanities and social-science shift that treats the archive as research site, theoretical concept and object of critique, often foregrounding the relation between archive and power [@Cifor2017CriticalFeminismArchives, pp. 13–17].

- **Situated knowledge:**  
  I will use this, via their discussion of Haraway, to frame archival knowledge as produced from positions and relations rather than from neutrality or disembodied objectivity [@Cifor2017CriticalFeminismArchives, pp. 8–9].

- **Community:**  
  I will treat this as a politically charged and uneven formation, not an automatically benign unit of archival value or access [@Cifor2017CriticalFeminismArchives, pp. 19–21].
