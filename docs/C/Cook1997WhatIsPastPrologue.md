## Claim 2
- **Claim (plain):** Cook argues that the archival profession has shifted from a state-centred, custody-based model toward a wider socio-cultural and societal role grounded in memory, accountability, and public use.
- **Evidence (quote/paraphrase + page):** In the prologue he frames archives as “houses of memory” implicated in the politics of remembering and forgetting, asking whose memory is preserved and who is excluded. Later, in Conclusion I, he identifies as the first broad theme a shift “from a juridical-administrative justification” grounded in the state “to a socio-cultural justification for archives grounded in wider public policy and public use,” adding that archives are now “of the people, for the people, even by the people.” `[@Cook1997WhatIsPastPrologue, pp. 17–19, 43–44]` :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}
- **Warrant (my words):** This matters because Cook is scaling archival theory outward. Archives are no longer justified only as evidence for administrations, but as infrastructures of collective memory, accountability, identity, and culture.
- **So what for my thesis:** Cook gives me a strong basis for arguing that reactivating the DDR archive is not a narrow source-recovery exercise but part of a broader archival responsibility to public memory and contested knowledge.

## Claim 3
- **Claim (plain):** Cook’s positive proposal is that archival work is moving from product to process, from physical custody to contextual intervention, and from passive keeping to active shaping.
- **Evidence (quote/paraphrase + page):** In Conclusion I he identifies several converging themes: the shift from the physical record to the functions and transactions that cause records to be created; the need for archivists to intervene actively in appraisal, description, and electronic recordkeeping; and the fact that archivists have evolved from “passive keepers” to “active shapers of the archival heritage.” In Conclusion II he makes the future-oriented claim most explicitly: provenance should be redefined around functions and processes, archives should move from “product-focused to a process-oriented activity,” and the resulting framework is “postcustodial.” `[@Cook1997WhatIsPastPrologue, pp. 44–49]` :contentReference[oaicite:7]{index=7}
- **Warrant (my words):** This is the paper’s strongest contribution for your project. It gives a direct historical warrant for archive activation, provenance-aware computational work, and contextual modelling as archival work rather than as departures from it.
- **So what for my thesis:** Cook provides one of the clearest archival justifications for treating the computational strand as part of archival process and contextualisation, not merely as an external technical layer added onto old records.
