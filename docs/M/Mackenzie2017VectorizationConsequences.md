# Thesis job

**How the source moves the primary research question forward:** Mackenzie argues that machine-readable vector space is actively *made* by transformations of heterogeneous materials into numerical, geometrically operable forms. For the DDR thesis, this establishes a critical question upstream of retrieval and visualisation: what happens to the provenance, difference and possible meanings of an archival trace when its text becomes an embedding and its modelled relationships become navigable?

**How it bears on the secondary question:** A contemporary semantic comparison may help identify DDR-period ideas worth revisiting, but the comparison's mathematical basis cannot itself tell us whether the ideas were historically connected, accepted, contested or influential. A design-historical argument must establish those matters from appropriate records.

**Why read it now:** Chapter 1 frames machine learning as a heterogeneous knowledge practice and Chapter 2 establishes the movement of diagrams among code, mathematical notation and infrastructures. Chapter 3 takes one indispensable transformation—**vectorization**—as its object. It is the most direct of these chapters for interrogating the interpretative status of the DDR text embeddings, semantic atlas and retrieval workflows.

**My benchmark:** I will treat this theoretical argument as useful when (1) I can show, for a chosen DDR trace, which operations and modelling assumptions turned source text into a searchable vector and what provenance continues to be available; and (2) a researcher can inspect and test a proposed semantic relationship against the underlying records, including plausible counterexamples and absent/poorly retrieved traces.

# Position, evidence and argument

Mackenzie starts by claiming that the vector space is an expanding **epistemic space**, not a natural location where data were already waiting (pp. 51–52). The transformation from mixed, situated records to vectors can be obscured by the modest phrase 'data cleaning' (pp. 51–52). He then reads the table through Foucault's *The Order of Things* and through the very different datasets of *The Elements of Statistical Learning* (pp. 53–59). The prostate example provides a sustained material case: a numerical table, pairwise scatterplot matrix, categorical encoding, multi-dimensional regression, code and statistical outputs all reorganise what can be said about the data (pp. 59–72). At the close, the ability to compare by distance and direction rather than shared origin appears as the key epistemic consequence of vectorization (pp. 73–74).

This argument concerns **tabular data, linear algebra, categorical encoding, regression and vectorised programming** as illustrated by statistical machine-learning practice up to 2017. Mackenzie is **not** describing present-day multilingual text embeddings, the bge-m3 model, UMAP, or retrieval-augmented inference. The applications below are expressly **my inferences**, to be tested against the DDR research instrument and archive rather than attributed to Mackenzie.

# The author's central move

Mackenzie proposes an **archaeology of data shapes**. Diverse empirical materials are ordered into tables, encoded as numerical vectors, mapped into spaces with many dimensions, acted upon by models and code, and sometimes re-presented as diagrams or new statistical tables. These operations permit wider forms of comparison and classification; at the same time, they transform the visibility and local meanings of the materials involved (pp. 51–52, 59–63, 73–74). The critical task is to study the *operations of passage* rather than declaring vectorization inherently reductive or treating the vector space as neutral.

# Nine-claim critical evidence ledger

## Claim 1 — Vectorization is a constitutive transformation, not merely a cleaning step

**Author claim:** Machine learners encounter differently generated things—numbers, words and images—only after they have been put into compatible, specifically structured vector, matrix or array forms. Mackenzie argues that describing this work as 'data cleaning' conceals consequential operations (pp. 51–52). **Evidence-supported claim:** The opening directly identifies axes, coordinates, scales and the remaking of row–column grids as the practices to be examined (pp. 51–52). **Warrant:** The analytical space is produced from the source material by particular operations; it is not a transparent copy of the material's original arrangement. **My inference:** In the DDR instrument, scanning/OCR, identifying PID-backed documents, making text chunks, selecting an embedding model and creating an index need to be narrated as a sequence of derived representations. These are not all the same operation, and neither OCR nor chunking should be mislabelled 'vectorization'. **Boundary:** Mackenzie's examples establish a conceptual problem; they do not prove which particular DDR transformation introduces error or whose records become less accessible. **Consequence:** The thesis should document the source-to-vector chain with explicit selection and transformation decisions, rather than beginning its method account at the map or generated answer. **Practice cross-check:** Compare a canonical DDR source, its extracted text, PID and chunk representation, and the indexed vector record. **TODO (attach exact PID, corpus release, model/version and processing receipt).** [@Mackenzie2017MachineLearnersArchaeology, pp. 51–52]

## Claim 2 — A common mathematical space makes heterogeneous cases comparable without erasing their origins

**Author claim:** Machine learning brings otherwise distant settings into a repeatable framework of numerical operations. **Evidence-supported claim:** **Table 3.1** lists disparate datasets—clinical measurements, spam, handwriting, speech, marketing and more—carried together in one machine-learning textbook (pp. 53–55). Mackenzie asks *how* such forms are ordered and homogenised rather than accepting their juxtaposition as natural (pp. 54–55). **Warrant:** Common operations make the comparison possible, but the empirical objects and institutional practices that generated those datasets remain heterogeneous. **My inference:** Semantic neighbourhoods can legitimately prompt inquiry across DDR reports, teaching materials and archival descriptions whose documentary genres differ; the shared embedding space provides a *computational basis for comparison*, not evidence that all genres share a historical context, authorship or evidential weight. **Boundary:** A single vector representation does not guarantee that cross-genre similarity is reliable. The table of unrelated training examples is not evidence that the particular DDR embedding model treats all of its documentary genres adequately. **Consequence:** Evaluate retrieval and neighbourhoods by documentary type, include source-genre metadata in interpretation, and avoid claims of historical equivalence from numerical proximity. **Practice cross-check:** Test matched and deliberately dissimilar DDR document genres in the semantic atlas and retrieval UAT. **TODO (record queries, distance/rank values, source passages and expert verdicts).** [@Mackenzie2017MachineLearnersArchaeology, pp. 53–55]

## Claim 3 — The table is a historically contingent ordering device, not an innocent store of facts

**Author claim:** Mackenzie repurposes Foucault's account of changing tabular order to understand how machine-learning tables both inherit and transform earlier practices of classifying difference. **Evidence-supported claim:** He contrasts a Classical grid that renders identities and differences simultaneously visible with a later conception in which life, labour and language have historical structures not exhausted by tabular enumeration (pp. 56–57). He then suggests that machine-learning tables operate on heterogeneous material through new, expandable relations of similarity (pp. 57–58). **Warrant:** A table has an ordering logic—what can be placed in rows and columns, what counts as a variable, and what relationships are exposed—that can affect how knowledge is produced. **My inference:** The DDR archival catalogue, the machine-ready corpus and its vector index are three non-identical systems of order. A historically meaningful relationship may appear in one and be unavailable or misleading in another. **Boundary:** Mackenzie's Foucauldian genealogy does not itself document the RCA/V&A archival catalogue, nor establish that a table or catalogue field has suppressed a particular person. Foucault's historical account is the framework Mackenzie *uses*, not an empirically verified history of every tabular format. **Consequence:** The theoretical framework should distinguish archival arrangement and description from computational remapping, then test the difference with concrete examples. **Practice cross-check:** Compare original catalogue associations, records and derived metadata for a named DDR project or actor. **TODO (provide record identifiers and evidence for any visibility/absence claim).** [@Mackenzie2017MachineLearnersArchaeology, pp. 56–58]

## Claim 4 — The 'epistopic' concerns where universal operations meet local data practices

**Author claim:** Borrowing Mike Lynch's term, Mackenzie calls the vectorised table **epistopic** because local situations are brought into contact with general criteria such as validity, precision, confidence, error, uncertainty and likelihood (pp. 58–59). **Evidence-supported claim:** He explicitly defines the epistopic as an intersection between a local complex of activities and broader epistemic themes; the statistical table remains connected to its locally produced context even as algebraic operations proceed (pp. 58–59). **Warrant:** Calculability alone does not establish that a result is meaningful in the field from which the data came; it acquires a situated warrant, or fails to do so, through examination of those local circumstances. **My inference:** My DDR model may return a technically plausible association, but its historical warrant must be established separately through sources, metadata, attribution and temporal positioning. This is a more precise conceptual account of why the RAI answer needs provenance and an explicit distinction between source testimony and generated synthesis. **Boundary:** Mackenzie does not specify an archival evidence hierarchy or evaluate RAI claim validation. **Consequence:** Make every computational result open to a local, document-specific evidential test instead of assigning model confidence the role of historical certainty. **Practice cross-check:** Use one production UAT answer with its evidence cards, claim check and source passages. **TODO (identify the UAT item and compare each asserted relation to its cited page).** [@Mackenzie2017MachineLearnersArchaeology, pp. 58–59]

## Claim 5 — A new visualisation reveals some relations while other relations require a different model

**Author claim:** The transition from the table to a scatterplot matrix changes what is perceptible, but neither arrangement exhausts the multivariable relations that the dataset might contain. **Evidence-supported claim:** **Table 3.2** and **Figure 3.1** present the same prostate dataset as rows of measurements and as multiple pairwise plots (p. 60). Mackenzie observes that relations become visible through plotted pairings but that higher-order combinations cannot be read stably from the two-variable panels; different analysts may reach different conclusions (pp. 60–62). He describes the variables becoming dimensions of an otherwise not directly visible higher-dimensional space (p. 62). **Warrant:** Visual form selectively enables interpretation; a striking plotted relation is conditional on which variables, dimensions and views have been made visible. **My inference:** The DDR UMAP atlas is an explicitly *derived* low-dimensional view of a modelled semantic space. Clusters may invite useful questions, but the displayed geometry should be interrogated alongside text retrieval and direct source reading. **Boundary:** Mackenzie's scatterplot matrix is not a UMAP projection, and this chapter does not establish UMAP's exact preservation behaviour, parameter sensitivities or fidelity. Those belong to UMAP's own method literature and empirical tests. **Consequence:** Provide pathways from plotted points to source evidence, and test whether different retrieval or visual settings sustain the proposed interpretation. **Practice cross-check:** Compare a visually close DDR document pair against original passage content and a second view or retrieval configuration. **TODO (save atlas settings/screenshots and a documented human interpretation).** [@Mackenzie2017MachineLearnersArchaeology, pp. 60–62]

## Claim 6 — Encoding qualitative categories as numerical dimensions changes the terms of comparison

**Author claim:** To bring qualitative/categorical differences within algebraically operable space, machine learners create numerical codes and, where useful, additional dimensions. **Evidence-supported claim:** On pp. 62–63 Mackenzie explains dummy-variable representation: a category with multiple levels can be expressed as several binary indicators. This allows operations normally used for measured numerical quantities to be applied to categories, while the categories' original differences of kind recede within vector calculations (p. 63). **Warrant:** Encoding is a choice about representational equivalence and permitted operations, not a merely neutral formatting step. **My inference:** In DDR, the categories for staff roles, projects, genres or periods must retain their documentary definitions if ever encoded for comparison; embedding a paragraph is a *different process* from one-hot encoding a catalogue category, although both show that representation can alter what comparisons are possible. **Boundary:** The chapter does not establish that contemporary learned text embeddings are constructed using this specific dummy-variable procedure. Nor does recoding prove that the underlying category was historically false. **Consequence:** Where the instrument combines categorical metadata and vectors, specify the route by which each field is represented and keep the historical description available for examination. **Practice cross-check:** Inspect a DDR category such as department, project role or document type in catalogue form and in any downstream filter/model representation. **TODO (confirm whether any DDR categorical fields are numerically encoded and cite the code/metadata schema).** [@Mackenzie2017MachineLearnersArchaeology, pp. 62–63]

## Claim 7 — A fitted model creates a new object of analysis, whose adequacy cannot be read from fit alone

**Author claim:** Linear algebra maps a multivariable table into relations describable by coefficients, predicted values, vectors and hyperplanes that cannot simply be displayed as a complete everyday graphic. **Evidence-supported claim:** The regression formulae (eqs. 3.1–3.3, pp. 65–67) and **Figure 3.2** (p. 66) describe transformations and fitted surfaces in a high-dimensional space; the R model operations in **Listing 3.2** (p. 70) issue a new table of estimates, uncertainty and test statistics in **Table 3.3** (p. 71). Mackenzie's footnote to the clinical scatterplot discussion also reports that later urological work challenged PSA's value as a marker (p. 61, note 5). **Warrant:** An internally organised statistical output can be precisely produced yet require further domain evidence to judge what it means or whether the framing of its target is sound. **My inference:** A ranked DDR retrieval result or generated relation is likewise a new analytical object with provenance and methodological dependencies, not the historical event or intention itself. **Boundary:** Statistical regression estimates and their significance tests are not equivalent to neural text-embedding similarities or LLM answers; the PSA caveat is a useful source-specific warning about domain validity, not a general proof that all fitted models are misguided. **Consequence:** State what each computational score, projection or citation card actually measures or attests; reserve historical assertions for source-based corroboration. **Practice cross-check:** Use a UAT case in which a high-ranked retrieval fails to support an attractive cross-document inference and record the bounded verdict. **TODO (attach UAT question, source IDs and model release).** [@Mackenzie2017MachineLearnersArchaeology, pp. 61, 65–67, 70–71]

## Claim 8 — Vectorised programming reshapes practical work while concealing some of its execution

**Author claim:** Vectorization operates both as a representational transformation of datasets *and* as a coding practice in which a compact operation is applied across a collection. **Evidence-supported claim:** **Listing 3.1** on p. 68 contrasts a loop with vectorised addition, and the text explains that the apparent simultaneity depends on optimised low-level code or hardware (pp. 68–69). Mackenzie insists that the difference is not simply speed, but a changed way of working with whole arrays and transformations (p. 69); he connects those practices to GPU, cluster and other infrastructures (p. 70). **Warrant:** An elegant high-level operation can make consequential underlying choices and dependencies unobtrusive while changing the user's sense of control over data. **My inference:** I need to distinguish two senses relevant to DDR: the **embedding of text into learned numerical representations** and the **vectorised operations by which data are processed or searched**. Even if the interface makes query/retrieval seem like one action, the pipeline includes separate software and model operations. **Boundary:** Mackenzie's R code is an illustration of array programming, not evidence about the internals of a particular modern embedding service; the OCR of Listings 3.1–3.3 also contains malformed characters, so I have *not* run or certified that source code. **Consequence:** Show model/service boundaries and tested version information; do not describe high-level API use as equivalent to transparent knowledge of its underlying operations. **Practice cross-check:** Examine model/runtime receipts and retrieval service implementation for one DDR release. **TODO (provide component revisions and a reproducible test trace).** [@Mackenzie2017MachineLearnersArchaeology, pp. 67–70]

## Claim 9 — In vector space, distance can displace resemblance and provenance as the apparent basis of belonging

**Author claim:** A decisive effect of vectorization is to provide a common operational space in which similarities are organised through distance, proximity and transformative relations rather than requiring shared origin or readily recognisable resemblance. **Evidence-supported claim:** Mackenzie's conclusion explicitly states that vectorization mixes local realities, and that identity and difference in vector space are recalculated through measures of proximity/distance (pp. 73–74). He adds that the higher-dimensional volume cannot itself be shown adequately on a page or screen and that the resulting loss of visibility challenges critical thought (pp. 73–74). **Warrant:** A new comparative capacity can generate unexpected questions while also inviting false transfer of authority from numerical relatedness to contextual meaning. **My inference:** This is the central caution for the DDR semantic atlas and RAI: semantically neighbouring passages are *candidate traces* for investigation, not automatically testimony of influence, collaboration or causality. The scientific and archival value lies in what the researcher can subsequently establish, contest or delimit. **Boundary:** Mackenzie does not say that provenance must disappear or that mathematical distance has no value. Nor does he give a technical account of the distance metric, training corpus or dimensionality-reduction settings of any DDR component. **Consequence:** Present each semantic relation with source links, documentary status, and uncertainty; distinguish 'no retrieved trace in this bounded corpus' from 'there was no historical relation'. **Practice cross-check:** Compare three outcomes for the same scoped DDR question—retrieval-supported relation, ambiguous nearby material and a missingness result—with direct source inspection. **TODO (record exact production UAT cases and decisions).** [@Mackenzie2017MachineLearnersArchaeology, pp. 73–74]

# Definitions and distinctions for my framework

- **Vectorization (Mackenzie's broad archaeological sense):** A suite of operations that reform heterogeneous data into structured, algebraically operable vector spaces (pp. 51–52, 59–63, 73). **My use:** An analytic lens on the translation from archival text to a machine-ready numerical representation; I must still separately define the particular embedding process used by DDR.
- **Vector space:** A mathematical space whose coordinates, dimensions and operations permit transformation and model-fitting over datasets (pp. 59, 62–65). **My use:** The modelled representational domain in which document similarity can be calculated, *not* the geographical or historical arrangement of an archive.
- **Epistopic (Mackenzie via Mike Lynch):** The encounter between a local data-producing activity and general epistemic questions such as error, precision, expectation or confidence (pp. 58–59). **My use:** A prompt to inspect whether a computational relation earns its warrant from the underlying DDR record.
- **Table as space of order:** A system that makes selected identities and differences enumerably visible, historicised through Foucault's account (pp. 56–58). **My use:** A way to interrogate the distinct ordering work of archive catalogue, corpus metadata and vector database without assuming they are identical.
- **Dummy-variable encoding:** A way to express categorical variables as numerical binary indicators (pp. 62–63). **My use:** A case of representational transformation that must *not* be conflated with learned language embeddings.
- **Dimensional expansion/reduction:** Practices that add, change or compress coordinates in the representation (pp. 62–64, 73). **My use:** A conceptual basis for distinguishing original embedding dimensions from a later exploratory projection; UMAP mechanics require a separate technical source.
- **Vectorised code:** A coding convention in which operations are expressed across arrays/vectors rather than enumerated element-by-element in source-language loops (pp. 68–69). **My use:** A distinct infrastructural/programmatic sense of vectorization, not interchangeable with text embedding.
- **Similarity as proximity:** Relations expressed by distance/position in vector space rather than necessarily by common historical origin (pp. 73–74). **My use:** Candidate relevance for exploration, never sufficient proof of historical association.

# Critical response

The intellectual force of this chapter lies in its sustained attention to an operation that technical discussions often take for granted. **Table 3.2 and Figure 3.1 (p. 60)** are worth keeping beside the narrative: the *same clinical dataset* changes its available patterns and questions when presented numerically or as paired plots. The new higher-dimensional volume (pp. 61–62), encoded categories (pp. 62–63), fitted mathematical surfaces (pp. 65–67), code (pp. 68–70) and statistical output (p. 71) are then successive material moves, rather than metaphors for the same unaltered facts. **Figure 3.2 (p. 66)** makes the difficulty of depicting those transformations visually palpable. For a practice-led doctoral study concerned with representation, that chain is more useful than an unqualified claim that 'the algorithm is biased' or 'data are constructed'.

I would also retain several criticisms rather than uncritically recruit Mackenzie as methodological authority. First, 'vectorization' is stretched from reshaping categorical data into coordinates to the idiom of vectorised programming and even infrastructural arrangements (pp. 62–70). The connections are interesting, but their different mechanisms must be retained to prevent a catch-all explanation. Second, his broad Foucauldian suggestion of a revived or post-order table (pp. 56–58, 72–73) is provocative and historically framed rather than itself a comparative empirical test across archives or data systems. Third, the clinical example includes a domain-validity qualification—later doubts about PSA as a biomarker (p. 61, note 5)—that sharply limits any temptation to treat successful statistical modelling as settled substantive knowledge. Fourth, the conclusion's claim that similarity comes to rely on distance rather than genesis (p. 73) is especially strong: it helps explain a danger of reifying computational associations, but does not establish that every modelled association *lacks* meaningful underlying historical relation. The research task is to test rather than presume either outcome.

**Reusable thesis sentence:** *Vectorization gives the DDR corpus a new space of computational comparability, but the proximities it produces remain method-dependent propositions for historical inquiry, not archival evidence of relationship until corroborated through attributable records.*

# Practical integration: test an alleged archival relationship

**Proposed demonstrator:** Choose one question that joins named DDR people, ideas or projects. Trace **source record and archival description → OCR and PID-backed document → text chunk → learned embedding and its model version → retrieval/neighbourhood ranking → UMAP or other designed view → cited interpretative proposition**. At each transition record what is newly calculable, what contextual information survives, what is no longer apparent on the interface, and which historically relevant alternatives remain untested. Then compare the proposed relation with the documents themselves. This method maps Mackenzie's concern with transformation to *an inspectable research procedure*; it is not claimed to be his own prescribed protocol.

**Specific proposed evaluation:** Hold the historical question constant and contrast (a) a retrieved semantically near passage with a verifiable documentary relation; (b) a near passage with common vocabulary but no support for the proposed relation; and (c) a bounded corpus query that finds no adequate support. Those three results would let the thesis discuss usefulness, false association and scoped missingness without making the semantic distance do the evidential work. **TODO (choose cases, record query text, PID set, model/pipeline versions and UAT adjudication).**

**Theoretical-lens interfaces:** Critical archival theory must establish how the original record, descriptive arrangement and missingness shape what exists to be retrieved. Critical design historiography must judge whether DDR concepts and actors were historically connected or in tension. Feminist and situated critique must examine whether recorded attribution and computational legibility reproduce uneven labour visibility. Mackenzie's operational archaeology supplies a way to *question* the computational mediation; it cannot substitute for those inquiries.

# Boundary and misuse risk

**Boundary:** This chapter is an archaeological and conceptual reading of statistical modelling, table-based datasets, regression and vectorised programming as practised in the 2010s. It does not test contemporary generative language models, bge-m3, UMAP or DDR provenance design.

**Risk:** Its most memorable idea—that distance reshapes similarity—could be used as an automatic indictment of semantic search or, conversely, as a grand justification for every proximity map. Either move would evade the bounded, case-specific evidential testing that the chapter's own attention to operations recommends.

# Cross-chapter and cross-lens synthesis

Chapter 1 established the programme of investigating machine learning as an historically and materially heterogeneous practice of knowledge production; Chapter 2 proposed diagrammatic movement among code, equations, images and institutions as a way to work within that practice. Chapter 3 now identifies a particularly consequential hinge: heterogeneous material becomes mutually calculable when remade as vectors and acted upon in a shared space, but this generates new forms of similarity while making the original differences and the complete mathematical geometry harder to inspect (pp. 51–52, 62–63, 73–74). For the DDR PhD, this changes what 'activation' can responsibly mean: semantic search and projection can suggest new lines of historical questioning, while source records, catalogue histories and testimony must remain capable of confirming, complicating or rejecting each proposed relation. It strengthens the **critical computational approaches** lens at the very point where it meets archival description, design historiography and situated critique, and supports a **working proposition** that a designed source-to-vector-to-evidence pathway can generate knowledge both *about DDR* and *about the mediated conditions under which DDR becomes computationally legible*.
