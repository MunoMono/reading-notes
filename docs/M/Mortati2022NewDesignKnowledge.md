# Thesis job

**How this source moves the primary research question forward:** Mortati gives a contemporary account of design knowledge as historically mutable and tied to changing objects, agents, data and problem structures, creating a bridge for asking which DDR-era propositions can be reactivated without assuming that their original conditions still hold.

**How this source bears on the secondary question:** It directly serves the forward-looking question by proposing which design capabilities matter under contemporary learning systems: problem setting, visual intelligence, mixed data, socio-technical transformation, interdisciplinary collaboration and ethical responsibility.

**Why I’m reading this now:** It offers a present-day comparator for the DDR period’s debates around design knowledge, systematic method, systems, computing and the status of design as a distinctive mode of inquiry.

**Where it sits in my argument:** Critical design historiography as a contemporary bridge: the paper helps me compare historical formulations of design knowledge with a current attempt to redefine the discipline’s objects, agents and epistemic practices.

**Why this theoretical-framework area + literature cluster is the right filing location:** Although the paper is contemporary theory, its usefulness here is historical-comparative: it explicitly updates inherited orders and principles of design and therefore provides a controlled way to ask what has changed since the DDR period.

**My benchmark for using it:** I will use Mortati’s fifth-order model as a present-day proposition to compare with DDR ideas, not as a universal stage theory; and I will test whether the historical traces support specific continuities before claiming lineage.

# Position + moment

Mortati writes from a period in which AI, data-driven technologies, sustainability transitions and global socio-technical challenges are presented as changing what designers need to know. She frames design as potentially bridging technological research, socio-cultural and ethical responsibility, and innovation, while arguing that design knowledge must be reconsidered as designed outputs become dynamic, learning and more-than-human systems. [@Mortati2022NewDesignKnowledge, pp. 21–23]

# The author’s main move

Mortati extends Buchanan’s orders of design into a proposed fifth order in which data are both input and output, non-human agents participate in design, and the designer’s task shifts toward problem setting, socio-technical transformation, visual knowledge-making and the stewardship of evolving learning systems rather than the delivery of fixed, near-perfect solutions. [@Mortati2022NewDesignKnowledge, pp. 23–34]

# Critical-reading claims

## Claim 1

**Claim.** Contemporary changes in technology and society require a reformulation of design knowledge rather than merely an expansion of the designer’s toolkit. **Author claim.** Mortati argues that AI, digital transition, sustainability and systemic challenges confront design with problems that exceed established disciplinary boundaries and twentieth-century educational models. **Evidence.** She describes four orders of challenges—performance, systemic, contextual and global—and argues that these require knowledge models that move beyond established disciplines toward responsive reformulations of practice. [@Mortati2022NewDesignKnowledge, pp. 21–22] **Evidence-supported claim.** The source supports treating design knowledge as historically contingent on the kinds of problems, systems and responsibilities designers face. **Researcher inference.** DDR-era definitions of design knowledge should therefore be revisited by testing both what persists and what contemporary conditions make inadequate. **Warrant.** If the objects and conditions of designing change, inherited epistemic models cannot be assumed to transfer unchanged. **Boundary.** Mortati does not empirically test whether current design education is universally anchored in obsolete models. **Consequence.** The secondary research question should evaluate historical ideas proposition by proposition against changed contemporary conditions. **Practice cross-check.** TODO (identify DDR propositions about design knowledge and test which assumptions depend on industrial-era problem structures).

## Claim 2

**Claim.** The object of design is shifting from passive output toward evolving systems populated by multiple agents. **Author claim.** Mortati draws on more-than-human and stakeholder-centred approaches to argue that outputs are no longer passive, finished solutions but systems that learn and change over time, with algorithms and other entities exercising agency within the design process. **Evidence.** She describes designed systems as dynamic, learning outputs and identifies agential, temporal and infrastructural shifts that alter participation, responsibility and control. [@Mortati2022NewDesignKnowledge, pp. 22–23] **Evidence-supported claim.** The paper supports a move from object-centred design toward relational systems in which human and non-human agents participate in outcomes. **Researcher inference.** This creates a productive contemporary contrast with DDR debates about systems, computing and human judgement. **Warrant.** When outputs themselves change through use and data, the designer cannot fully specify the finished state in advance. **Boundary.** Mortati’s account is a theoretical synthesis and does not establish that all contemporary design has become more-than-human or adaptive. **Consequence.** Historical claims about control, optimisation and final solutions should be examined against whether they presuppose passive and stable design outputs. **Practice cross-check.** TODO (compare DDR system/process models with the behaviour of the thesis research instrument as an evolving socio-technical system).

## Claim 3

**Claim.** Mortati’s fifth order makes mixed data and non-human participation central to design rather than peripheral technical concerns. **Author claim.** She defines the fifth order as a condition in which mixed types of data are both input and output, while people, machines and micro-organisms can all participate in reaching design objectives. **Evidence.** Mortati explicitly presents the fifth order as characterised by data centrality and by design’s need to engage equally with people and other “species,” alongside ethical questions about experience and business models. [@Mortati2022NewDesignKnowledge, p. 23] **Evidence-supported claim.** The fifth-order proposition expands the locus of design agency and the evidential materials on which design operates. **Researcher inference.** For my thesis, computational representations can be treated as designed participants in inquiry only if their status remains distinguishable from the archival evidence they transform. **Warrant.** Data-driven agents may shape what is visible and actionable without becoming equivalent to source evidence. **Boundary.** Mortati’s category is a conceptual “thinking tool and compass,” not a demonstrated historical law of disciplinary progression. **Consequence.** I can use the fifth order as a comparative device while resisting claims that the thesis instrument itself proves a new stage of design. **Practice cross-check.** TODO (document where computational outputs alter inquiry conditions while source provenance remains inspectable).

## Claim 4

**Claim.** The meaning of the “artificial” is recast from manufactured form toward learning agents and data-dependent systems. **Author claim.** Mortati argues that Simon’s industrially rooted artificial should be reconsidered because contemporary artificiality includes algorithms, data-fed learning agents and relationships in which agency, participation, power and control are distributed. **Evidence.** She states that “artificial” now includes learning agents interacting with people and the data that feed those agents, and that these participants can become centres of design innovation. [@Mortati2022NewDesignKnowledge, p. 25] **Evidence-supported claim.** The source supports a historical shift in what design’s artificial objects are understood to comprise. **Researcher inference.** This offers a precise contemporary test for DDR-era claims that defined design in relation to artefacts, systems or artificial environments. **Warrant.** If the designed entity is itself adaptive and data-dependent, a definition premised on stable manufactured objects captures only part of contemporary practice. **Boundary.** Mortati’s updating of Simon is interpretative; it does not establish that Simon’s concept cannot be extended in other ways. **Consequence.** The thesis should distinguish ideas that remain conceptually elastic from those whose original object assumptions now need revision. **Practice cross-check.** TODO (compare historical DDR uses of “system,” “artefact” and “artificial” with the contemporary computational research instrument).

## Claim 5

**Claim.** The fifth order extends systems design toward learning, transformation and decentring the human. **Author claim.** Building from Buchanan’s four orders, Mortati proposes a fifth order in which mathematically engineered models, nature, micro-organisms and other species participate in systems whose behaviours evolve over time. **Evidence.** Her five-order map moves from graphic, industrial, interaction and systems concerns toward transformations characterised by socio-technical systems, data feedback loops and pathways to transformation; she says human relationships lose their former centrality. [@Mortati2022NewDesignKnowledge, pp. 26–28] **Evidence-supported claim.** The model marks a shift from designing systems around human interactions toward designing conditions in which heterogeneous agents co-produce outcomes. **Researcher inference.** This is useful for testing whether DDR systems thinking anticipated some relational concerns while remaining historically different in its technological and ethical assumptions. **Warrant.** Similar vocabulary such as “systems” does not imply equivalent ontology or agency. **Boundary.** The model is synthetic and normative rather than a comprehensive empirical history of design practice. **Consequence.** Any claim of continuity from DDR systems thinking to contemporary computational design must specify what is continuous and what has changed. **Practice cross-check.** TODO (compare selected DDR systems propositions with the fifth-order dimensions of agency, data, temporality and transformation).

## Claim 6

**Claim.** Fifth-order design knowledge requires designers to work across “thick” and “big” data rather than privileging one evidential mode. **Author claim.** Mortati contrasts qualitative, contextual “thick data” with large-scale data used to identify patterns and train algorithms, arguing that their merger becomes a central fifth-order problem. **Evidence.** She describes thick data as context-rich and often tacit while big data support panoramic behavioural patterns and algorithmic learning; she also presents data physicalisation as a way to turn data into contextual, manipulable and conversational learning environments. [@Mortati2022NewDesignKnowledge, pp. 28–30] **Evidence-supported claim.** The paper supports a plural data practice in which scale, context and representation do different epistemic work. **Researcher inference.** My archival method similarly needs documentary close reading and computational patterning to remain distinct but interoperable. **Warrant.** Pattern detection and situated interpretation answer different questions and carry different risks. **Boundary.** Mortati does not address archival provenance or historical source criticism directly. **Consequence.** Computational views of DDR should remain linked to the underlying traces and should not replace contextual interpretation. **Practice cross-check.** TODO (test how embedding/UMAP patterns change when read back against source passages, metadata and testimony).

## Claim 7

**Claim.** Designing learning systems turns feedback loops and interdisciplinary collaboration into core design material. **Author claim.** Mortati argues that AI-based services expose capability uncertainty and output complexity, and that systems with feedback loops require designers and data scientists to work holistically across data, algorithms, interfaces and user experience. **Evidence.** She describes the difficulty of prototyping adaptive AI behaviour and cites feedback-loop design as requiring close designer–data-scientist partnership. [@Mortati2022NewDesignKnowledge, pp. 30–31] **Evidence-supported claim.** The source supports treating data pipelines, algorithms and interfaces as one socio-technical design problem rather than as separable implementation stages. **Researcher inference.** The thesis instrument should therefore be documented as a joined epistemic system in which corpus construction, retrieval, inference and interface shape one another. **Warrant.** If a system learns or adapts through feedback, the boundaries among technical and experiential components affect the resulting behaviour. **Boundary.** Mortati discusses product/service learning systems, not historical-research systems. **Consequence.** I can borrow the integrative design principle while keeping archival evidence and machine inference epistemically separate. **Practice cross-check.** TODO (use versioning and UAT logs to show where data, model and interface decisions jointly altered research behaviour).

## Claim 8

**Claim.** Visual intelligence is treated as a form of design knowledge because representations make complex problems thinkable and shareable. **Author claim.** Mortati argues that design knowledge is embodied in cognitive artefacts—visions, scenarios, technical representations, diagrams and other sense-making devices—and that visual thinking should move increasingly toward problem setting and understanding systemic complexity. **Evidence.** Her discussion of design knowledge distinguishes stored information from embodied knowledge, then describes visual representations as cognitive artefacts through which designers manipulate, communicate and generate knowledge. [@Mortati2022NewDesignKnowledge, pp. 32–33] **Evidence-supported claim.** The source supports treating representational practice as epistemic work rather than communication after the fact. **Researcher inference.** The thesis’s diagrams, semantic maps and interface views can be evaluated as research instruments that structure inquiry, provided they do not acquire evidential authority beyond their source data. **Warrant.** A representation that changes what can be perceived, compared or questioned participates in knowledge production. **Boundary.** Mortati’s account does not provide criteria for validating historical visualisations. **Consequence.** Visual outputs should be documented with provenance, design decisions and limits as part of the method. **Practice cross-check.** TODO (cross-reference semantic atlas and provenance-interface decisions that changed what relations could be inspected).

## Claim 9

**Claim.** The fifth-order shift moves design from perfect problem solutions toward provisional problem seeking, inclusivity and ethical stewardship. **Author claim.** Mortati argues that learning systems make “good enough, for now” more relevant than near-perfect fixed outputs, that problem seeking becomes more important than problem solving, and that design knowledge must better integrate race, class, gender, sexuality, ability and planetary challenges. **Evidence.** The closing section explicitly contrasts industrial-era perfection/problem-solving with provisional learning systems, visual problem seeking, broader categories of need and the need to confront economic models that constrain ethical and sustainable design. [@Mortati2022NewDesignKnowledge, pp. 33–34] **Evidence-supported claim.** The source proposes a normative redesign of design knowledge around provisionality, problem framing, inclusion and responsibility. **Researcher inference.** This gives the secondary RQ a strong present-day test: DDR ideas about systematic inquiry may remain useful where they support evidence and problem framing, but they require revision where they imply closure, neutrality or narrow stakeholder models. **Warrant.** Historical usefulness depends on how an idea behaves under contemporary ethical and socio-technical conditions, not simply on whether its vocabulary survives. **Boundary.** Mortati’s closing recommendations are normative and do not demonstrate their adoption across professional practice. **Consequence.** The thesis should distinguish historical continuity from contemporary desirability and make value judgements explicit. **Practice cross-check.** TODO (evaluate whether the research instrument represents uncertainty, exclusion and provisionality rather than only answer production).

# Definitions / terms this changes

- **Fifth order of design →** Mortati’s thinking tool for design concerned with transformations, learning systems, mixed data and heterogeneous agents rather than only objects, interactions or static systems. [@Mortati2022NewDesignKnowledge, pp. 23, 27–28]
- **Learning system →** a socio-technical system that evolves through data and feedback, producing changing relationships, values and practices over time. [@Mortati2022NewDesignKnowledge, pp. 30–31]
- **Visual intelligence →** the use of cognitive artefacts and representations to manipulate, communicate and generate design knowledge, increasingly for problem setting and systemic sense-making. [@Mortati2022NewDesignKnowledge, pp. 32–33]
- **Good enough, for now →** a principle of provisional adequacy appropriate to evolving learning systems rather than fixed near-perfect solutions. [@Mortati2022NewDesignKnowledge, p. 34]

# My response

Mortati is most useful to the thesis as a controlled contemporary counterpoint. Her argument demonstrates that the question “what counts as design knowledge?” remains unstable, but that its instability now concerns agents, data, feedback, ethics and temporality in ways different from the DDR period. I should therefore use the fifth order to sharpen comparison, not to redescribe DDR retrospectively in contemporary terms.

**Reusable thesis sentence:** Revisiting DDR design knowledge requires distinguishing durable epistemic commitments—such as evidence, problem framing and representational reasoning—from historically specific assumptions about stable objects, human agency and the possibility of finalised solutions.

# Boundary + risk

**Boundary:** The fifth-order model is a contemporary theoretical proposal and compass, not an empirically established universal sequence of design history.

**Risk if misused:** Applying it retrospectively could make DDR appear to be an incomplete version of contemporary design rather than a historically situated field with its own contested categories and purposes.

# Cross-source / cross-lens synthesis

Boyd Davis and Gristwood show Archer and Lansdown moving away from fixed, linear computational models toward interactive, feedback-rich and context-sensitive accounts of designing; Mortati proposes a contemporary environment in which those issues have intensified because data-fed learning agents participate directly in outcomes. The connection is therefore not a simple lineage but a productive historical contrast around feedback, evidence, interaction and problem formulation. For the thesis, Mortati makes it possible to ask which DDR commitments remain generative under contemporary computational conditions and which require revision because agency, data, ethics and temporality have changed. That judgement still has to be made against specific DDR traces rather than inferred from conceptual resemblance.
