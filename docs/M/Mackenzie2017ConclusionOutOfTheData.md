# Thesis job

**How the source advances the primary question:** Mackenzie's affirmative but deliberately provisional argument for **critical operational practice** gives me a justification for making and interrogating a computational research instrument as part of the work of understanding the DDR archive. A system designed to suggest, display, test and sometimes refuse archival interpretations can produce research knowledge about the mediation of testamentary traces, provided it exposes its operations and does not mistake computational association for historical proof.

**Relation to the secondary question:** Re-examining DDR-era ideas through a newly designed instrument may change the questions contemporary design researchers can ask. That prospect is not itself evidence that any particular DDR method is current or valuable; the comparative historical and design-critical work remains to be undertaken.

**Why this conclusion matters now:** After eight close readings, I need to settle what I actually take from the book rather than borrowing its terminology without methodological commitments. The conclusion sums up Chapters 2–8 as seven facets of a machine-learning operational formation (pp. 211–213), then argues for an experimental involvement in practices of code, representation, writing and evaluation (pp. 213–218).

**My benchmark:** I will count Mackenzie as useful if I can (1) provide an **inspectable, versioned source-to-output DDR case** in which making or changing the instrument changes what can be asked or established; and (2) show in the same case how documentary testimony, computational selection, generated conjecture and human historical judgement remain explicitly distinct, including a failed or uncertain result.

# Context and central argument

Mackenzie begins with **two awkward sentences generated from his own book** by a character-sequence long short-term memory recurrent neural network (p. 209). Their quasi-technical language is recognisable enough to resemble the source, yet it does not straightforwardly constitute a warranted argument. The display is not a figure with a number; it is a typographic experimental artefact printed before the prose of his conclusion. He then revisits the book's opening question: if machine learning alters the production of knowledge, might critical thinking have to alter its empirical and theoretical practices? His answer is expressly **provisional**: working *within* learning operations might turn prediction and classification into opportunities for critical experimentation (p. 209).

He situates that prospect in a heterogeneous field of researchers, papers, code, infrastructures and subject positions (pp. 210–211). He recapitulates seven operational concerns—diagramming, vectorization, function finding, probabilization, patterns/differences, referential materialization and subject positions (pp. 211–213)—before turning to **in-situ hybridization**, technologies of the self, and **critical operational practice** (pp. 213–216). The close (pp. 216–218) confronts repetition, platform power, unstable model behaviour and the possibility that research through operations can produce a modest but meaningful difference in what is knowable and revisable.

**Author's central move:** A critique of machine learning should neither attribute all agency to algorithms nor remain wholly outside their operations. Mackenzie proposes experimental participation in a material and institutional field whose rules, regularities and contradictions can be documented and possibly altered (pp. 209, 214–218). This is a **normative and methodological proposal** grounded in his experience of learning and experimenting; it is not a proof that critical practice invariably produces emancipatory outcomes.

## Claim 1 — Generated resemblance is itself a critical experiment, not evidence of understanding

## Claim 2 — Critical machine learning is a practice of experimentally changing the critic's own method

**Author claim:** Mackenzie returns to his initial problem of how altered knowledge production might alter critique and answers **provisionally affirmatively** (p. 209). He proposes learning machine-learning operations to make the field's diagrammatic relationships available for experiment, including changes to the researcher's conduct and ways of thinking. **Evidence-supported claim:** His opening formulation connects the question to Stengers's 'experimentation of concepts' and describes prediction as a potential technology of critical experimentation rather than merely ordering/ranking (p. 209). **Warrant:** A practice may function critically when its technical experiments reveal assumptions and prompt accountable revisions to questions or methods, not simply when its outputs classify more things. **My inference:** In my DDR study, a designed semantic atlas and source-constrained RAI instrument can be a **method of inquiry into mediation**, rather than only a search service, if I document how particular implementations affected my judgement or enabled better historical questions. **Boundary:** This claim does not imply that building an app automatically constitutes practice-led doctoral knowledge, nor that every modification of a UI is epistemically important. Mackenzie claims *possibility*, not demonstrated universal success. **Consequence:** Keep dated evidence of a design change, the source-level question it motivated and what the ensuing test established or could not establish. **Practice cross-check:** Select a documented change to a DDR retrieval, source card or visual control, link journal/commit records and report the changed historical interpretation; **TODO (versioned case)**. [@Mackenzie2017MachineLearnersArchaeology, p. 209]

## Claim 3 — The field's statistical and technical scale is evidence of an operational formation, not one autonomous actor

**Author claim:** Mackenzie interprets a literature population of at least 230,000 unique authors and 146,000 publications as evidence of a heterogeneous human–machine operational field of scientific statements, devices and professional positions (p. 210). **Evidence-supported claim:** The counts are explicitly those of the particular research-literature corpus he has been drawing on; the prose locates authors and publications inside networks of settings, code, diagrams and infrastructure, with mutable boundaries between human and machine operations (p. 210). **Warrant:** The published success of an algorithm cannot be understood apart from distributed intellectual labour and technical arrangements that make its outputs possible. **My inference:** The DDR digital archive instrument must name human participants, source producers, cataloguers, the technical pipeline and current interpreters rather than narrating activation as something an autonomous AI 'did'. This also raises an attribution question across the original DDR historical archive and today's research project. **Boundary:** The 230,000 and 146,000 figures should **not** be repeated as present-day totals, full employment statistics or evidence about design researchers. They refer to Mackenzie's own literature corpus, whose sampling and indexing methods constrain their interpretation. **Consequence:** Maintain provenance and contributor distinctions both for historical source statements and for computational transformations. **Practice cross-check:** Use a DDR named-person/project evidence trail and a build/provenance record to identify who made and who later interpreted each statement; **TODO (specific source, PID, contributor record)**. ## Claim 4 — Diagrammatic critique differs from simply internalising machine processes

The ensuing recapitulation describes movements among datasets, equations, statements, code and infrastructures as diagrammatic work (pp. 211–212). **Warrant:** The book's methodological investment is in *actively representing, experimenting with and revising relations* among heterogeneous components; it need not posit an emotionally merged human–machine self. **My inference:** The graphic-design and interface part of the DDR thesis can contribute by exposing the steps from archival record to machine-made association and historical interpretation, not merely by producing an attractive atlas of clusters. **Boundary:** The argument is a disagreement with Wilson's proposed route to affective expansion, not a general finding that emotion or human affect is irrelevant to computational practice. ## Claim 6 — An operational practice can be critical without promising domination or perfect control

**Author claim:** In 'In-situ hybridization', Mackenzie locates ethical and political inquiry *within* operational formations and asks how practitioners might discover desirable changes rather than simply imagine gaining control over dominant platforms (pp. 213–215). **Evidence-supported claim:** The chapter discusses asymmetries of access, engages Mark Hansen's proposal for creative feed-forward relations (p. 213), adapts Foucault's technologies of self (pp. 213–214), and lays out **five considerations**: acknowledging one's implicated position, understanding plural power–knowledge relations, locating regularities and indeterminacies, allowing collective individuation, and recognising the possibility of transforming both self and collective (p. 214). He describes the resulting possibilities as limited and open rather than predetermined (pp. 214–215). **Warrant:** Critical intervention needs knowledge of specific conditions and points of slippage inside a system, not claims to external purity or universal control. **My inference:** My DDR instrument can be critically useful when it makes the scope of sources, uncertain relationships, inadmissible synthetic claims and excluded/uncaptured traces visible enough to contest the system's output. **Boundary:** Technologies of the self address practice and subjectivity; they are **not** a software validation technique. Mackenzie does not demonstrate that provenance safeguards will make archives ethically complete or distribute historical visibility equally. **Consequence:** Treat refusals, unresolved questions and documented missingness as *inspectable research outcomes* rather than errors to be hidden for the sake of a seamless demonstration. **Practice cross-check:** Compare PASS, evidence-guard refusal and scoped-missingness production UAT responses, with primary source links and human verdicts; **TODO (case IDs, reviewer and corpus scope)**. ## Claim 8 — Norms of verification and attribution can be displaced by performance unless made explicit

**Author claim:** Mackenzie closes by noting the extensive reproduction of standard learning tasks and the fascination of animating data transformations, while identifying a possible alteration of existing norms of *verification, objectification and attribution* (pp. 216–218). **Evidence-supported claim:** On p. 216 he remarks on recurrent example tasks such as spam classification and on the homogeneity of much research practice. On p. 217 he describes both learners' failures to generalise and the persuasive force of their dynamic optimisation displays. The sentence completed on p. 218 explicitly says that modelling and imputation can unevenly replace inherited norms of verification and attribution; he then prefers the possibility of diagrammatic reworking over endlessly repeating standard implementations (pp. 217–218). **Warrant:** Apparent performance can acquire social or epistemic authority independently of what a model has actually verified. The interesting critical possibility lies in making that substitution visible and disputable, not in rejecting all computational comparison. **My inference:** The DDR instrument's strongest defensible contribution may be to make **unsupported historical statements harder to publish**, even where a model can narrate them plausibly: source-level claim admissibility and visible uncertainty should take priority over fluency or an aesthetically polished visual cluster. **Boundary:** This is a proposed standard for the DDR instrument, not an empirical finding established by Mackenzie's book. His observation that verification norms shift does not mean one can restore historically objective certainty merely by adding citations. **Consequence:** Evaluate and report the difference between technical relevance, stylistic coherence, provenance traceability and genuinely supported historical inference. **Practice cross-check:** Select one cross-document DDR UAT answer that sounded compelling but exceeded source evidence, then document the production rejection, remedy and independent review; **TODO (actual evidence packet and production result)**. # Key terms and working uses

- **Critical machine learner (Mackenzie's proposal):** A situated practitioner who learns and diagrammatically experiments with machine-learning operations as an occasion for critical inquiry (p. 209). **My use:** An attitude of accountable making, testing, describing and criticising the DDR instrument—not a claim that the instrument itself is a human historian.
- **Operational formation:** The heterogeneous field of people, knowledge, devices, settings, statements, techniques and infrastructures in which machine-learning claims and subjects become possible (pp. 210–213). **My use:** A way to interrogate what enables DDR computational evidence, *not* a substitute for source provenance.
- **Diagrammatic process:** Relational transformation and representation spanning equations, code, diagrams, datasets and material practice (pp. 211–212, 215). **My use:** Draw and inspect each source-to-output transition, marking which are epistemically consequential.
- **Seven planes / facets:** The conclusion's synthesis of Chapters 2–8: diagrams, vectorization, operation/observation, probabilization, patterns/difference, referential materialization, and subject positions (pp. 211–213). **My use:** Optional critical questions rather than a technical pipeline or new overarching framework.
- **In-situ hybridization:** Mackenzie's heading for engagement from *inside* an operational formation, including transformative practices of self and collective (pp. 213–215). **My use:** A methodological disposition of working inside the system while critically accounting for its power and limits; not a literal transfer from the chapter's biological metaphors to archival processing.
- **Technologies of the self:** Borrowed from Foucault in the conclusion to ask how practices alter thought and conduct (pp. 213–214). **My use:** A reflective layer in the design/research journal; not an automatic certification of software quality.
- **Critical operational practice:** The combination of technical participation, code, writing and conceptual experimentation directed toward finding possibilities for difference and critique (pp. 215–216). **My use:** The most directly relevant methodological descriptor for critically making and testing the DDR instrument.
- **Verification/objectification/attribution:** Norms that Mackenzie suggests may be displaced as algorithmic estimation and modelling become persuasive (pp. 217–218). **My use:** Reasons to preserve source evidence, distinguish model proposals from recorded action and report uncertainty in answers.

# Critical assessment

The conclusion succeeds in closing a methodological circuit that began with the possibility of thinking critically **with** machine learning. The generated-text display on **p. 209** is the chapter's important visual/material artefact: it places a model's awkward recombination of the author's vocabulary directly into the book's typography, making the text itself a case of the operations under discussion. This is an experiment in representation and authorship, **not** a figure of historical proof. The comprehensive return to Chapters 2–8 on **pp. 211–213** is useful because it prevents the method collapsing into a slogan about diagrams or computation: mathematical transformation, probabilistic judgement, data objects and changing speakers must be analysed together but remain distinguishable. The sections 'In-situ hybridization' (**pp. 213–215**) and 'Critical operational practice?' (**pp. 215–216**) then provide the most directly usable justification for practice-led theoretical work: by working within a heterogeneous technical formation, the researcher can potentially make its constraints and openings critically visible.

Yet Mackenzie's conclusion is deliberately less demonstrative than his earlier technical case chapters. **First**, the claim that experimentation might transform critical conduct is explicitly **provisional** (p. 209): how much social, institutional or scholarly change occurred because of this particular experiment is not measured. **Second**, the return to large literatures and an extensive technical collective (p. 210) shows scale and participation, but a sample of authors and publications cannot, on its own, establish a complete account of unseen labour or uneven access. **Third**, the shift to care of self, collective individuation and freedom (pp. 213–215) offers a normative trajectory but few independently specified tests by which to judge when a technically fluent critical experiment has truly diverged from existing interests. **Fourth**, the seductive display of model activity and its effect on verification/attribution (pp. 216–218) diagnoses a substantial issue without proposing an archival provenance protocol. I need critical archival theory and my own source-level evaluation to do that additional work.

**Reusable thesis sentence:** *Working within computational techniques as a practice of critical operational inquiry allows the DDR archive to be reconfigured into new questions, but those questions become historically defensible only when the designed transformations, source provenance, interpretative judgements and unresolved limits are available for scrutiny.*

# How I will use the whole book

**Do not turn Mackenzie's archaeology into a fourth computational pipeline or replace the existing theoretical framework.** His strongest role is to justify and discipline the **critical computational approaches** lens within the already established four-lens framework.

The book contributes a coherent set of demands. Chapters 1–2 ask me to examine machine learning as an assembled practice and to diagram its operations. Chapter 3 requires an account of what is changed when an archival text becomes a numerical representation. Chapter 4 distinguishes procedures that transform data from observers of their success and error. Chapter 5 warns that corpus abundance does not end selection or uncertainty. Chapter 6 shows how patterns depend on specific techniques of differentiation. Chapter 7 questions how a selected/regularised model constitutes a new object of inquiry. Chapter 8 places human and computational producers and evaluators in changing positions of attribution and authority. Chapter 9 converts this analytic repertoire into a **critical-practice commitment**—make, examine, test and potentially revise one's own computational operations.

For the DDR thesis, the operational chain I will scrutinise is **archival original and description → digitised document and PID → extracted text/chunk → embedding and modelled similarity → retrieval or atlas representation → tentative generation/synthesis → source-level corroboration and human historical interpretation**. This is **my DDR workflow**, not a diagram prescribed by Mackenzie. For every transition I should be able to ask what changed, who decided, what was excluded, what remains source-traceable, and what authority the resulting statement may legitimately claim.

**Two essential demonstrations:** (1) one successful but initially non-obvious DDR relationship that the instrument made investigable and historical evidence substantiated; and (2) one superficially compelling but unsupported or missing relationship whose evidential limits the instrument and researcher could expose. These must come from fixed production UAT and source packets, with PIDs, dates, model versions, outputs and adjudications (**TODO**).

# Boundary and risk

**Boundary:** This is the closing methodological argument of a 2017 book, based partly on the author's own technical experiments and historical examples. It is not a modern architecture or evaluation manual for bge-m3, UMAP, Qwen, retrieval-augmented inference, archival authenticity or C2PA credentials.

**Risk:** Borrowing 'critical operational practice' as an elegant epithet for a visually impressive interface without evidence that making and using it changed an interpretative question; attributing historical insight to a similarity score; equating a model-generated text with testimony; or suggesting critical proximity to computational practice cancels questions of institutional power or invisibility. Each would turn a provisional method into an unsupported research finding.

# Cross-chapter and cross-lens synthesis

The conclusion changes the thesis problem from **how to use a machine-learning system to recover hidden DDR relationships** into a more defensible question: **how can designing and critically operating a computational instrument make the formation, selection and interpretation of proposed archival relationships themselves available for inquiry?** Mackenzie's seven intersecting facets (pp. 211–213), his practice-based closing method (pp. 215–216), and his concern with inherited norms of verification and attribution (pp. 217–218) strengthen **critical computational approaches** by furnishing a method of inspecting *how* source-derived representations become evidence claims, without granting the model the status of an archive witness. This lens depends on **critical archival theory** for provenance, archival description, material loss and authority; on **critical design historiography** for adjudicating DDR-era contested knowledge; and on **feminist and situated knowledge** for questions of omitted or unevenly recognised authorship and labour. The new working proposition is that **critical archival activation is the design and testing of accountable conditions for inquiry, including the refusal of unsupported relations**, rather than unrestricted generation of connections. That proposition must still be demonstrated by specific, production-state DDR evidence packets, documented design decisions, counterexamples and interpretative outcomes (**TODO**); the book supplies its critical vocabulary and methodological invitation, not its historical proof.
