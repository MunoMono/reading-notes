# Position + moment (2–4 lines)
Mordell writes from critical archival studies and digital archives practice in 2019, at a moment when datafication, computational archival science, NLP, and “collections as data” were becoming increasingly prominent. Her essay is both conceptual and interventionist: rather than rejecting archives-as-data outright, she asks what digital archives become when they are reframed as data and what this means for archival identity, labour, and politics. She positions the issue explicitly against Terry Cook’s paradigms and asks whether a fifth paradigm — archives-as-data — is emerging. `[@Mordell2019CriticalQuestionsArchivesData, pp. 140–146]` :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}  
**Canon assumptions to problematise / update for 2026 (1–2 lines):** This text is strongest when used to keep computational archival work critically reflexive. Its key warning is that datafication can quietly reactivate older myths of neutrality, objectivity, and natural order precisely when archivists think they are innovating. `[@Mordell2019CriticalQuestionsArchivesData, pp. 146–151, 156–161]` :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

# The author’s main move (1 sentence)
They try to map the emerging archives-as-data paradigm by analysing how datafication and computational methods can both enable new archival access and, at the same time, conservatively revive notions of neutrality, objectivity, and rawness unless actively challenged. `[@Mordell2019CriticalQuestionsArchivesData, pp. 144–147, 156–161]` :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}
