## Claim 2
- **Claim (plain):** Grigg argues that provenance offers a better basis for finding primary sources than the older legal metaphor of testimony.
- **Evidence (quote/paraphrase + page):** She criticises the common reduction of primary sources to “testimony” about events, arguing that this metaphor privileges intended communication and slights nontextual, incidental, or contextual evidence. She then redefines a primary source as “any surviving material that is generated or altered in the course or as an outcome of that activity or provides a context for its occurrence.” From there she argues that the “genetic model,” which directs attention to the context in which sources were created, is a better basis for search than the metaphor of testimony. `[@Grigg1991ArchivalPracticeFoundations, pp. 230–232]`
- **Warrant (my words):** This is the essay’s key conceptual shift. By replacing testimony with provenance and activity, Grigg broadens what can count as a primary source and makes search depend on structures of action, not just on explicit statements.
- **So what for my thesis:** Grigg gives me a strong basis for treating DDR traces as products of activity, not just as explicit testimonies or finished statements, which is especially important for diagrams, administrative files, project records, and marginal materials.
- **Practice cross-check:** This aligns strongly with Patterson on interface work, with your interest in testamentary traces, and with the need to read records, reports, diagrams, and institutional paperwork as evidence of activity rather than only of overt declaration.
