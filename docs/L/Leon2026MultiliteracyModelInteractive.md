# The author’s main move (1 sentence)
They try to redefine interactive visualization literacy as a multiliteracy model spanning goals, tasks, tools, workflows, interaction, devices, perception, interpretation, and insight in order to explain how people actually use interactive visualizations and where they struggle. `[@Leon2026MultiliteracyModelInteractive, pp. 1–2, 4–8]` :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}

## Claim 1
- **Claim (plain):** León et al. argue that interactive visualization literacy is not one thing but a set of distinct literacies distributed across an action cycle.
- **Evidence (quote/paraphrase + page):** They define interactive visualization literacy as “the ability to explore data through interactive visualizations” through an iterative process of “formulating goals, performing interactions, and evaluating their results.” They then construct a 3×3 model combining three levels of abstraction with three gulfs, producing nine multiliteracies. ## Claim 2
- **Claim (plain):** The model’s strongest insight is that interaction problems happen at different abstraction levels: operational, tactical, and strategic.
- **Evidence (quote/paraphrase + page):** On pages 5–7 the paper defines the operational level as immediate interface actions, the tactical level as concrete sequences of tasks to obtain a visualization state, and the strategic level as high-level planning and insight generation across longer analytical processes. The opening figure on page 1 also visualises these levels stacked from operational through tactical to strategic. `[@Leon2026MultiliteracyModelInteractive, pp. 1, 5–7]` :contentReference[oaicite:8]{index=8}
- **Warrant (my words):** This matters because it stops “usability” from being reduced to clicking correctly. A person may know how to manipulate an interface yet still fail to form the right analytical goal, choose the right task, or integrate findings into insight.
- **So what for my thesis:** This model helps me separate different kinds of difficulty in archive activation: trouble using the interface is not the same as trouble forming a historical question, and neither is the same as trouble generating an interpretation from the results.
- **Practice cross-check:** This is highly useful for evaluating any DDR visual analytics prototype, since users may struggle at the level of device use, task selection, or strategic interpretation for very different reasons. TODO (needs evaluation note cross-link).

## Claim 3
- **Claim (plain):** León et al. argue that interactive visualization design and education should explicitly scaffold these literacies rather than assume them.
- **Evidence (quote/paraphrase + page):** In the discussion and research agenda they recommend discoverability cues, interaction explanations, onboarding, direct manipulation, standards, alternative interactions, and support for strategic actions such as notebooks and history functions. # Definitions / terms this changes (only the ones that matter)
- **Interactive visualization literacy (IVL):** the ability to explore data through interactive visualizations by formulating goals, performing interactions, and evaluating results; I will use this term when discussing the demands of visual analytics interfaces rather than static charts. `[@Leon2026MultiliteracyModelInteractive, pp. 1–2]` :contentReference[oaicite:10]{index=10}
- **Multiliteracy model:** a model that treats interactive visualization literacy as a set of distinct but connected literacies rather than one monolithic competence; I will use this to describe why archive activation must be evaluated across several skills. `[@Leon2026MultiliteracyModelInteractive, pp. 1–2, 4–8]` :contentReference[oaicite:11]{index=11}
- **Visualization action cycle:** León et al.’s adaptation of Norman’s action cycle for interactive visualization, combining levels of abstraction with gulfs of execution, evaluation, and goal formation; I will use this as a way to frame user movement through archive interfaces. `[@Leon2026MultiliteracyModelInteractive, pp. 4–7]` :contentReference[oaicite:12]{index=12}
- **Analysis literacy / task literacy / tool literacy:** the goal-formation literacies that operate at strategic, tactical, and operational levels; I will use these terms where users need to formulate questions, break them into tasks, and understand what a system can do. `[@Leon2026MultiliteracyModelInteractive, pp. 7–8]` :contentReference[oaicite:13]{index=13}
- **Workflow literacy / interaction literacy / device literacy:** the execution literacies covering complex multi-stage analysis, specific interaction techniques, and device use; I will use these when analysing how users navigate archive tools. `[@Leon2026MultiliteracyModelInteractive, pp. 7–8]` :contentReference[oaicite:14]{index=14}
- **Insight literacy / visualization literacy / visual literacy:** the evaluation literacies spanning high-level analytical synthesis down to perception of visual elements; I will use these to separate perception from interpretation and interpretation from insight. `[@Leon2026MultiliteracyModelInteractive, pp. 8–9]` :contentReference[oaicite:15]{index=15}
